## Best fix per scenario (opus)

- **S2**: Add a CSV output rule to the generated prompt: 'Return exactly one line with 8 comma-separated fields in the H2 order, no header, numbers with 2 decimals and no thousand separators, and any field containing a comma in double quotes. If a value is missing, leave that field empty (nothing between the commas) and put the [ASK] under Needs you, never inside the row. An empty or unclear field is a Fail for H1, not a Can't tell. Write the field count as evidence for H2.'
- **S6**: Replace the generic input placeholder with the fields this task always needs: PR number, PR URL, title, description, diff or changed files. Add one rule: checks about what the output contains (here H1 to H6) are Pass or Fail only, never Can't tell. If a needed field is missing, the check fails and the field is listed under Needs you.
- **S1**: When building the prompt, turn each part of the learner's 'Definition of good' into its own Pass/Fail check (here: states progress, names a risk and what it blocks, gives next steps with an owner or date). Then always append one standing hard line: 'Every fact in the draft is stated in the input; quote the input line for each.' The judge would then catch the seeded hard_fail and weak outputs, and claims like 'arrived late' would fail instead of slipping through.
- **S8**: Replace the vague input placeholder with two labelled slots the learner fills once and reuses for every run: (1) 'Bakery facts': price list, eggless options, delivery charges by area, order cutoff and lead times, slot availability; and (2) 'How I reply to regulars': 2 or 3 real past WhatsApp replies. Then add one line to the checks: 'Never confirm a date or slot unless Bakery facts says it is free; score Warmth only by comparing with the example replies, and use Can't tell if none are given.'
- **S3**: Replace the Input placeholder with three labelled slots: 'The talk notes', 'My one take: the claim from this talk I'd defend in the comments' and 'One past post of mine I liked'. Then add one task step: 'Build the whole post around My one take: open with it, support it only with points from the notes, cut every other point, and end with a question to readers. If My one take is empty, write [ASK: the one claim you'd defend] and mark Ready = No.' This fixes the 'one idea' failure, makes the hook something the model can draft without inventing it, gives Q2 a voice to match, and stops thin notes from counting as postable drafts.
- **S4**: Before building the prompt, automatically split any hard line that contains a person's approval or a future event into two parts. Make the checkable part ('every number matches the source export, recomputed and shown') the Must pass check, and move the approval part to 'Needs you'. Then make the input slot ask for the source export by name ('paste the source export / last deck here: H1 checks every number against it'). That way H1 can actually Pass and the 8-of-10 decision becomes measurable.
- **S7**: Before building the scorecard, rewrite each hard line and quality as a question about the output, for example 'Does every shortlisted person meet H2, and does every rejection cite the resume?' and 'Q1: how well does the shortlist's reasoning use the actual systems built?'. Move any line that needs a person's later judgement or a future event (like H1) into a separate 'Checked later by you' section that does not count toward 'Ready for your review'.
- **S5**: Before the generated prompt, have the page detect checks that only restate the goal (e.g. 'it is correct', '1 = bad; 5 = good', 'Good replies'). For these, it should show a warning and instruct the LLM to mark them 'Can't tell: check is not defined' rather than score them. Next to each one, put a concrete support-ticket example in plain language: 'Pass when the reply promises no refund or recovery the policy in the input doesn't allow'; '1 = ignores the customer's question; 5 = answers it, states the next step and who does it'. The learner then sees exactly why their scorecard is empty and what a gradable line looks like.

## Problems found

### S1 Complete and clean (Judgment, 2 hard lines, 2 qualities, grader checked)
The drafts were usable and honest about gaps, but the self-check could not tell good emails from seeded bad ones: no judge caught the hard_fail or weak_quality outputs, and haiku failed even the good one. The hard lines (under 200 words, names a risk) are too easy to pass. Q1 is about how the client reacts and Q2 assumes there is a delay, so the scores that matter came out generous or inconsistent.
- eval: The definition of good names progress, risks and next steps, but only risks became a hard line. A draft with no next steps or no progress still passes.
- eval: H2 'names at least one risk' is trivially satisfied. It does not require the risk to say what it blocks (UAT? go-live?) or by when, which is exactly the gap the haiku run left open while scoring Q1 a 5.
- eval: There is no hard line against stating facts the input does not support. The sonnet run said the firmware 'arrived late' when the input never said it had arrived.
- eval: There is no hard line against blaming the client. The tricky run wrote 'the data exports we received from Acme were wrong twice' to a client who was already annoyed, and nothing could fail it.
- eval: Q1's ends describe the client's reaction ('has to reply', 'knows where things stand'), which the model cannot observe. An observable version would be: every blocker says what it holds up and the new date.
- eval: Q2's anchors assume a delay exists. In a week with no delay the scale has nothing to score.
- eval: The population is ten sent emails, which are outputs, not inputs. To run 10 drafts the learner needs the notes or facts behind each of those weeks. The two emails the client pushed back on were never turned into checks for what went wrong.
- eval: The decision says '8 of 10 pass' but does not say whether pass means the hard lines only or also a minimum Q1/Q2 score. Two easy hard lines make 8 of 10 almost automatic.
- prompt: The definition of good is shown as 'a person judges it later, not you' instead of being split into checks. The model never verifies that progress and next steps are present.
- prompt: The prompt adds no standing check that every fact traces to the input. It relies on the 'Not from the input' list, which the runs did not use to catch 'arrived late'.
- prompt: Quality scales give only the 1 and 5 ends with no middle anchors. Models drifted to 4 or 5 (haiku Q1 5, haiku-tricky Q2 4 when 3 was right).
- prompt: The Can't tell rule is ambiguous for reader-reaction qualities. Runs split between scoring Q1 from the output and marking it human_or_future, and none explained the choice.
- prompt: Word counts were asked for but still off by about 2 in two runs. There is no instruction to list or count line by line, and no margin rule near the limit.
- prompt: The input placeholder is generic. It does not tell the learner which facts this email always needs: last week's email, current dates, owners, and what each blocker gates.
- prompt: The page gives no instruction on how to run the 10-draft decision. The scorecard reads as a verdict on one draft, and two graders had to point out that one draft cannot settle 8 of 10.
- run haiku/typical: The email is faithful to the input, about 108 words, and correctly leaves an [ASK] for the sign-off. The scorecard is mostly honest and flags the vendor-blame tone issue. However, Q1 at 5 is generous because the email never says what the scanner slip and the Pune VPN gap block (UAT? go-live?). Its 'helpful' date suggestion (3rd/4th) is wrong and would mislead the learner if followed.
- run sonnet/typical: The draft is accurate and well structured, the word count is honest (I count about 103), and the model used a placeholder for the sender name instead of guessing it. The one real slip is "arrived late", which tells Meera the firmware has been delivered when the input doesn't say so; the learner should fix this before sending, and one draft can't settle the 8-of-10 decision on its own.
- run haiku/tricky: It went right in three ways: it stuck to the facts, marked gaps with [ASK], left out the internal 'Meera is annoyed' remark, and said 'not ready'. It went wrong on tone: 'The data exports we received from Acme were wrong twice' puts the blame on the client and never owns the delay, so Q2 is a 3, not a 4. That matters with a client who is already annoyed. Smaller points: the scorecard does not say that Q1 really depends on how the client reads the email, the word count is about 86 rather than 84 (still a pass), and one draft cannot test the learner's 8-of-10 rule.
- run sonnet/tricky: The draft holds closely to the input. Every assumption is flagged, and the Meera omission and the unowned delay are handed back to the learner rather than papered over. Two small issues: the word count is off by a couple (about 70, not 72; the Pass still holds), and Q1's anchor is about how the client reacts, which the scorecard can only estimate, yet it never says that a likely reply trigger (does Acme need to resend the export?) is still open. Also note that this is one draft, while the learner's decision needs 8 of 10 drafts run against their past Sent-folder emails.

### S2 Rule task, no quality layer (hard lines only)
The runs were honest: no invented values, missing date and currency were flagged, and both tricky runs caught that the invoice's own line items (1,325.00) do not match its total (1,352.00). But the judge could not tell good outputs from bad ones (the good output got Fail or Needs you, the same as the seeded bad ones), and rows with [ASK] text inside them would not import, so the learner cannot use this eval to make the 9-of-10 decision.
- eval: H1 'Every field matches the invoice' does not say how to grade a field that is blank or a placeholder. One judge called that Can't tell and another called it Fail, so 'imports with no fixes' has no clear Pass.
- eval: H2 mixes two checks, column order and date format, so a row with '[ASK: date]' was marked Pass for order while it fails on format.
- eval: H2 does not say whether GST is one combined column (CGST+SGST or IGST added together) or split. The LLM had to guess and flag it as an inference.
- eval: There is no rule for currency when the invoice does not state it, such as 'INR unless shown otherwise'. The USD invoice in the population makes this a known gap.
- eval: There is no CSV rule that makes the row importable: no header row, exactly 8 fields, quote any field with a comma, no thousand separators, 2 decimal places. One run broke the row into 9 columns with a comma.
- eval: H3 does not say what to do when the invoice itself does not add up, as in the 1,325 vs 1,352 case. It also gives no rounding tolerance, such as within 0.01.
- eval: The scanned invoices are in the population, but no check covers misread characters in GSTIN or invoice_no. For example, a GSTIN should be 15 characters.
- prompt: Step 1 tells the LLM to write [ASK: ...] in the draft. For a CSV row that puts free text, sometimes with commas, inside an import field and breaks the row the eval exists to protect.
- prompt: 'Output: the finished work only, ready to copy' conflicts with the [ASK] placeholders, and the prompt never says to keep asks out of the row.
- prompt: There is no rule for how to grade a check when a field is a placeholder. The same output got Pass, Fail and Can't tell from different judges, which is why the good output could not be told apart from the seeded bad ones.
- prompt: The generic 'For any length limit, count and write the number' line does not fit here. What this task needs is 'count the fields and write the number', and that rule is missing.
- prompt: The input placeholder asks for 'prices, policies, numbers' but does not ask for what this task always needs: the GST column rule, the default currency and one correct example row.
- prompt: The 'expert at this task' framing just repeats the task line and adds nothing that helps.
- run haiku/typical: It did the right thing by refusing to guess the date and currency, and it disclosed the summed GST of 7470.00. But it marked H2 Pass even though the date field is '[ASK: date]' rather than YYYY-MM-DD, so the row as returned would not import. H1 is strictly a Fail, not Can't tell, because two fields are placeholders.
- run sonnet/typical: The LLM put [ASK] in the currency field instead of assuming INR. It also flagged the day/month date reading and the combined CGST+SGST figure as inferences, so nothing was silently guessed. The row will not import until the learner fills in the currency. The eval itself never says whether GST should be one combined column, so the learner should add that to H2.
- run haiku/tricky: The best part is that it refused to force H3 and caught a real 27.00 gap (1,325 vs 1,352, which looks like a transposed total) instead of inventing a subtotal. But the GST [ASK] text contains an unquoted comma ('confirm amount, or 0 for export'). That splits the row into 9 columns and pushes total and currency one place right, so H2 actually fails and the row would not import cleanly.
- run sonnet/tricky: The key win is catching that the invoice's line items (1,325.00) don't add up to its stated total (1,352.00), and refusing to invent a subtotal or GST to force H3 to pass. The row needs two values filled before it imports, and the self-scorecard honestly says so. H2 passes: there are 8 fields in order, the date is in YYYY-MM-DD, and the [ASK] notes contain no commas.

### S3 Judgment task, no hard lines (quality only)
The prompt kept the LLM honest: no invented facts in any run, and gaps were marked with [ASK]. But the drafts listed every talk point instead of one idea, the self-scores ran about a point high, and no judge could tell good outputs from seeded bad ones, so the learner can't use the scorecard to make the 8-of-10 'post as is' call.
- eval: There are no hard lines at all. Checks a reader can see, such as 'no hashtags or emojis', 'no claim the speaker didn't make' and 'ends with a question that invites comments', are either buried inside a 1-to-5 anchor or missing.
- eval: Q2 bundles four separate things (first person, short sentences, one idea, no corporate tone or emojis). A draft can meet three and miss one, and the scale has no way to show which one failed. Every run lost points on 'one idea' while passing the rest.
- eval: Q1's 5 anchor, 'a claim my peers would argue with', depends on how other people react. A grader can't see that, so it either guesses or answers Can't tell. Only the 1 anchor ('I'm excited to share', a generic opener) can be checked on the page.
- eval: The definition of good is about comments and people stopping to read, which happens later. Nothing in the checks covers the visible part of that, such as a closing question or a stated position.
- eval: The decision says 'drafts I'd post as is' but doesn't link that to scores (for example 'Q1 and Q2 both 4 or higher, no [ASK]'). So the 8-of-10 count is a separate gut call, not a reading of the scorecard.
- eval: The population assumes every set of notes contains a take worth posting. The tricky case had no opinion in it, and no prompt can turn that into a post someone would publish as is.
- prompt: The prompt never tells the drafter to pick one idea and build the post around it. So it walks through every point in the notes, which breaks Q2 every time.
- prompt: The input slot asks for 'facts this task always needs' but not for the two things this task actually needs: the learner's own take (the claim they would defend) and a past post in their voice.
- prompt: Asking for an arguable hook while also saying 'never make things up' pulls the model in two directions. Nothing tells it to look for the claim in the notes or to ask for one, so it either softens the hook or hedges.
- prompt: The general Can't tell rule makes the model mark the whole of Q1 as unknowable when part of it is observable. In one run it also under-claimed Q2 even though every Q2 anchor can be checked from the text.
- prompt: The 1-to-5 quality scores have no pass threshold, so 'Ready for your review: No if any check failed' never fires because of a quality score.
- prompt: 'For any length limit, count and write the number' is boilerplate here, since this eval has no length limit.
- prompt: The same model drafts and grades its own work, and it ran about a point generous. Nothing asks it to name the lowest anchor the draft still matches before giving a score.
- prompt: The definition of good is shown to the drafter but labelled as judged later, so no draft asked readers to comment.
- run haiku/typical: Every fact traces to the input and the scorecard admits engagement is unknowable, but the post just restates the five talk points in order, so it misses the 'one idea' anchor. The hook is a view many eval practitioners already hold, not one peers would argue with; 'no framework, a spreadsheet' or 'zero evals' would have made a stronger, more arguable opener. Q1 at 4 is one point generous.
- run sonnet/typical: Every fact traces to the notes, and the post meets the observable Q2 anchors: plain, short sentences, no emojis or hashtags. It loses a point on 'one idea' because it walks through five talk points. The scorecard was honest but under-claimed on Q2, since those anchors can be checked from the output alone. The draft also never invites comments, even though the definition of good asks for them.
- run haiku/tricky: The LLM did the right thing. It refused to invent what the learner said about cost, caching and evals, and asked for those points with [ASK] slots. It also marked the draft not ready. It scored itself one point high on both checks. The opener is a generic event line, which is a 1 under the rubric's own anchor. The post also covers three topics, so it fails the 'one idea' part of Q2.
- run sonnet/tricky: The LLM correctly refused to invent a hook, a caching point or an evals answer from notes that had none. It marked each gap with an [ASK] and admitted Q1 can't be scored, so the result is an honest skeleton rather than a postable draft. The learner's real fix is upstream: these notes don't hold enough content for any draft to count toward the 8-of-10 'post as is' decision.

- eval: Population 'Some tickets' names no ticket types, so tricky cases (refunds, angry customers, outages, missing account info) are never targeted.
- eval: Definition of good 'Good replies' only repeats the task. It says nothing about tone, accuracy against policy, or resolving the issue.

- eval: Only one hard line, so it never names the real support failures: promising refunds or recovery without approval, inventing account details, not asking for missing identifiers.
- prompt: The page passes empty checks straight through, so the LLM grades against 'Pass when it is correct' and '1 is bad. 5 is good'. Weak runs then give confident-looking scores (Q1 = 4, Q1 = 3) instead of flagging that the rubric is undefined.
- prompt: There is no instruction to cross-check the 'Not from the input' list against the draft line by line. Haiku wrote None while its draft said 'your account shows' and 'we will refund'.
- prompt: 'Needs you' items can still introduce new specifics, such as Sonnet naming order ID PRO-55121, which the input never gave. The no-invention rule only covers the output, not the scorecard.
- prompt: The input placeholder asks for 'prices, policies, numbers' generically. A support task needs the refund/escalation policy and the customer's account or order facts, and these were not prompted.
- prompt: 'Ready for your review: Yes' stays possible even when every check is Can't tell. A scorecard with no gradable checks should never read as ready.
- run haiku/typical: The scorecard was right to mark H1 Can't tell and to block sending with an [ASK]. ### S6 Overloaded (6 hard lines, 4 qualities)
The prompt reliably produces accurate, short, honest summaries, and the scorecard flags missing facts instead of inventing them. But H6 fails on every PR because no link is ever supplied, accuracy is not a gate, and the judges could not reliably tell good outputs from seeded bad ones, so the 8-of-10 rule cannot safely approve posting without a human edit.
- eval: H6 'it links the PR' can never pass: the inputs carry no URL, so all ten fail and the 8-of-10 bar is unreachable. The eval needs to say where the link comes from, either supplied in the input or built from the PR number and a repo URL.
- eval: Accuracy is only a quality score (Q1), not a hard line, yet the decision lets the bot post with no human edit. A summary that claims something the diff does not do could still pass all six hard lines and ship. 'Every claim is supported by the diff or description' should be a Pass/Fail line.
- eval: The decision counts passes but never defines one. It does not say whether a summary must pass H1 to H6 only, or also reach a minimum on Q1 to Q4.
- eval: H4 'mentions any migration or config change' passes by default on PRs with none, and it cannot be checked without the diff. It should read 'if the diff includes a migration or config change, it names it'.
- eval: Q4 sets a PM as the reader, but the definition of good says 'a reader who skipped the PR' in an engineering digest. Graders scored Q4 generously on summaries full of Redis, Postgres and env var names. The eval should pick one reader and say how much technical naming is acceptable.
- eval: Q3's top end, 'names the one thing to watch after deploy', has no answer for refactors and the revert. It also asks only for the thing to watch, not the consequence, and graders flagged that consequence as missing.
- eval: Q1 depends on the diff, but the population is described as PRs without saying the diff is included. Graders noted the diff was not visible, so accuracy could only be checked against the description.
- eval: H3 'one sentence first' is ambiguous. It could mean the first sentence states the change, or that the change is stated in a single sentence.
- prompt: The input slot is generic ('prices, policies, numbers'). It never asks for what this task needs: PR number, PR URL, title, description, diff or changed files, and any linked issue. This one gap causes both the H6 failures and the unverifiable Q1.
- prompt: The Can't tell rule ('information that is not in the input') leaks into checks about the output itself. Sonnet marked H6 Can't tell when the output plainly had no link. Checks about what the output contains should be Pass/Fail only.
