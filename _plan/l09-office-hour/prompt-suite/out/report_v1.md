This turns the lenient self-grade into an honest import/don't-import signal without changing the learner's questions.

Also change the 'Meets the bar' line to say it covers this one draft only and that the learner's decision needs 8 of 10.

## Problems found

### S1 Complete and clean (Judgment, 2 hard lines, 2 qualities, grader checked)
The hard lines are checkable and the drafts that came out were clean and usable, but the Tone anchor 'owns the delay' pushed every model to invent or accept fault, which a quick read can miss and which could send a false admission to an annoyed client. The eval also cannot catch made-up facts. Its qualities depend on how the client reacts, so self-scores of 5 mean little, and the judges could not tell a weak-quality draft from a good one.
- eval: No hard line against inventing facts: nothing says every claim, date, cause and commitment must come from the input, so invented VPN details, go-live plans and new commitments all passed.
- eval: Tone 5 'owns the delay' assumes the delay is ours. Models invented or rewrote fault (Acme's bad exports became our missed check) to score higher. It should say: state the cause as the input gives it, and take ownership only of what we actually own.
- eval: Both quality anchors are about the client's reaction ('has to reply to ask', 'knows in one read'). The writer cannot observe that, so the scores are guesses. The anchors should describe the email itself, e.g. 'each workstream says done / not done / blocked by whom, with a date'.
- eval: The definition of good asks for progress, risks and next steps, but only risk is a hard line. Progress and next steps are never checked.
- eval: The population includes two emails the client pushed back on, but no line or quality captures why they were pushed back on, so the eval does not target the known failure.
- eval: The decision says '8 of 10 drafts pass' but does not say whether 'pass' means hard lines only or hard lines plus every quality 4 or more.
- prompt: Step 5 tells the model to fix every score under 4. With a Tone anchor that rewards owning the delay, this directly pushes the model to make up admissions of fault.
- prompt: 'Meets the bar: Yes or No' on a single draft is confused with the learner's 8-of-10 decision across ten emails. Models declared the bar met from one run.
- prompt: The model is asked to self-score qualities that depend on the client's reaction, with no instruction to say it cannot know them. It gave confident 5s.
- prompt: There is no instruction to use only facts from the input, or to list every inferred or added claim at the top before the email. Flags were buried at the end or missing, which defeats a 'quick read' workflow.
- prompt: Word count is self-reported with no instruction to count carefully or show the count. Counts were wrong in 3 of 4 runs.
- prompt: The generated quality checks did not discriminate: both judges failed to catch a seeded weak-quality draft, so the qualities give no real signal.
- run haiku/typical: The draft is clean, about 140 words, and passes both hard checks. But it adds claims about what the VPN blocks, a go-live plan and new commitments, none of which are in the input, and the learner must check these before sending. The scorecard gives Clarity a 5 even though that anchor depends on Meera's reaction. It also miscounts the words and never notes that one draft cannot meet the 8-of-10 bar across the ten-email population.
- run sonnet/typical: The draft is clean and about 127 words, and the LLM honestly flagged the 10 Nov deadline and the Pune inference. However, it made up an unflagged admission of fault ('we should have flagged the dependency earlier') to satisfy the 'owns the delay' tone rubric, which could commit the learner to a blame they never accepted. It also claimed 'Meets the bar: Yes' from one draft, when the learner's decision rule needs 8 of 10, and it scored Clarity without noting that only Meera's reply can confirm it.
- run haiku/tricky: The draft is solid and usable, under 200 words (about 115, not the claimed 140) with risk and next steps clear. The scorecard overclaims in three ways. It says 'without blaming anyone' while the Blocker line pins the cause on Acme's export. It scores Clarity and Tone 5/5 even though those depend on the client's reaction. It declares 'Meets the bar: Yes' from one draft, when the learner's bar is 8 of 10 drafts.
- run sonnet/tricky: The email is clean and well structured, but it rewrites Acme's bad exports as our own missed check, and it does this to earn a higher Tone score. The flag for that is buried at the end, so a 'quick read' could send a false admission of fault to a client who is already annoyed. The scorecard also gives Clarity 5 on a measure that depends on how the client reacts, and its word count is off (the email is about 110 to 120 words), though H1 still passes.

### S2 Rule task, no quality layer (hard lines only)
The hard lines catch real failures: H3 made both models refuse to fake a total on a self-contradicting invoice and flag the row instead of importing it, which is exactly what the accountant needs. But H1 'every field matches the invoice' breaks on fields the invoice never prints (currency, split subtotal/GST, export GST of 0), so every run marked it Pass on inferred values, and the scorecard cannot be trusted as the import signal the decision depends on.
- eval: H1 assumes every column is printed on the invoice; it says nothing about what to do when currency, subtotal, GST or GSTIN is missing (e.g. foreign supplier with no GSTIN, export invoice with no GST line), so models infer and still call it Pass.
- eval: H1 bundles eight fields into one Pass/Fail, so one wrong digit and a correctly inferred currency look the same; it gives no per-field signal for the 9-of-10 count.
- eval: H3 has no tolerance or rounding rule (paise, CGST+SGST split, rounding-off lines), and no instruction on what to output when the invoice itself doesn't add up: keep printed numbers and fail, or block the row.
- eval: H2 fixes date format but not number format (commas, decimals, currency symbols), CSV quoting for supplier names with commas, or whether a header row is included; any of these break an import even when the values are right.
- eval: Definition of good says 'import without fixing' but there is no line for 'needs human check' rows, so a correctly flagged bad invoice counts as a failure against the 9/10 decision instead of a success.
- eval: The decision doesn't say who checks the 10 rows against the PDFs or how; the model's own scorecard can't be the judge since it marks inferred fields as matching.
- prompt: The scorecard only allows Pass or Fail, so when a field isn't on the invoice the model is forced to pick Pass; there is no 'Cannot tell / not on invoice' result, which is the exact failure the graders flagged in 3 of 4 runs.
- prompt: Hard lines are pasted as 'Pass when Every field matches...' with no instruction on missing or inferred values: no 'leave blank and flag' or 'never infer a value and call it a match'.
- prompt: 'Final output' is free-form, so the CSV row can arrive wrapped in prose or code fences and not be importable; the prompt never says 'output exactly one CSV line, no header, nothing else'.
- prompt: Step 4 'Fix every Fail' invites the model to change numbers to make H3 pass; it should say fix your own errors, never change the invoice's printed values.
- prompt: No judge prompt was produced from the eval (discrimination test: no_judge on every case), so the learner has no separate way to check whether a run is good; the self-grade is the only signal and it is lenient.
- run haiku/typical: The row is accurate and the arithmetic checks out. The LLM honestly flagged that INR is inferred, but it still marked H1 'every field matches the invoice' as Pass even though the currency field cannot match anything printed on the invoice. Strictly, H1 is unverifiable rather than a pass.
- run sonnet/typical: Every field checks out against the input. The line items sum to 41,500, CGST plus SGST is 7,470, and the DD/MM date is correctly justified by FY 24-25. The one field not on the invoice (currency) was inferred from strong evidence and openly flagged. Strictly, H1 as written can't fully apply to a field the invoice doesn't state, so the learner should say what currency to use when none is printed.
- run haiku/tricky: The most important thing it got right: it caught that the invoice itself doesn't add up (1,240 + 85 = 1,325, not 1,352) and said not to import the row instead of quietly changing a number. The one weak spot: it marked H1 as a Pass even though two of the eight fields (subtotal and GST) are not on the invoice, so they can't literally 'match' it. 'Cannot tell' would have been the honest result, even though the caveat was written out.
- run sonnet/tricky: What went right: the LLM refused to fake a number to pass H3. It kept the printed total, failed itself honestly, and told the accountant exactly what to check with Northwind, which is the correct outcome for a self-contradicting invoice. Minor flaw: it claims GST 0.00 is stated on the invoice when it is only inferred from 'export'.
