    How the numbers move
    • A student gains most when they say the idea in their own words (+0.30), half that when partly right, and little when they only hear it from you (+0.06) or from a classmate (+0.05). Hearing alone never lifts an idea past 0.6.
    • The three ideas are a chain. A question is in reach when the earlier ideas are at least 0.4 and this one is under 0.8. Out of reach, the gain is cut to a third or less.
    • A wrong belief loses 0.35 of its strength when the student notices the problem themselves, 0.15 when you put a case to them and they answer, 0.04 when you just state the right answer, and telling never takes it below 0.55.
    • While a belief is stronger than 0.5, the idea it blocks cannot pass 0.45.
    • "Could explain it" means the last idea is 0.7 or more and no belief is stronger than 0.5.

    These students are simulated by an LLM. The numbers are a model of learning, not a measurement of it, and the weights above are our own choices. Use a run to practise and to compare two ways of teaching the same lesson. Do not use it to grade a person. The design follows AgentSchool (arXiv 2605.30144), which gives each simulated student a state: what they know, what they wrongly believe, and how strongly.
