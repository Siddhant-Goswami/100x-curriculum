What to hand to an agent

An agent is fast, cheap and sometimes wrong. Here is how to decide what it does, and how far it runs alone, on one real week.

Scroll ↓

A consultant's week

Stays with you Accelerated: it does, you commit Autonomous: it runs alone New work it creates

Think of a week in a consultant's life

Any job is a bundle of tasks. An engagement manager in Bengaluru preps client meetings, writes research briefs, triages the inbox, drafts slides, builds the model and signs the recommendation. Then travel and the home loan on the side.

An agent can change each task in one of three ways, or leave it alone.

The tasks and minutes are an illustration, not a measurement.

First, measure one real week

Log each task as it ends: how often, how many minutes. Estimate nothing. You can only save time you measured.

Each square is now sized by hours a week. Briefs and slides are big; bills are a speck.

One task per line: "weekly client status email", not "reporting". Below once a week, setup usually costs more than it saves.

Some tasks stay yours

Three questions, and one no is enough. Could a junior sign the output, or is it a judgment you are held to? Can the agent reach the inputs? Can you check its work in two minutes?

Five fail. Three are judgments, bills sit behind portals it cannot reach, and checking the model takes as long as redoing it.

To see whether a work task is already done with AI at scale, look it up in the Anthropic Economic Index.

Everything else starts Accelerated

Seven tasks pass. The agent does the work and stops before the step that commits. It drafts, you send. It finds the flight, you book. It ranks the inbox, you act.

Accelerated is where every passing task begins, however good the agent looks in a demo.

A few earn the right to run alone

Two things must hold. A mistake is cheap: easy to undo, and it touches no client, money or anyone else's numbers. And the agent proved it: 8 of 10 runs on your own tasks, checked in the tool you use.

Meeting prep and sector monitoring qualify. Nothing client-facing does.

The bar is per task type and expires after 60 days, or sooner when the agent changes its model, connectors or pricing. A smarter agent moves no square; a cleaner undo does.

Handing work over creates work

Someone scores the test runs, and someone re-tests when the agent changes. That is new work, and it is yours.

The result: the week changes

About 10 hours a week come back, almost all from quiet, read-only work: meeting prep, research, monitoring. The judgments are as big as before.

That is the point. The hours go to the work only you can sign.

Keep the log running for a month after you hand anything over. A test cannot show multi-week reliability.

The framework on one card

Ask four questions of every task, in order. Each one only matters once the one before it holds.

  1. 1Did you log it for a real week?If not, log first
  2. 2Could a junior sign it, can the agent reach the inputs, and can you check it in two minutes?If not, it stays with you
  3. 3Is a mistake easy to undo and kept away from clients, money and other people's numbers?If not, Accelerated at most
  4. 4Did it pass 8 of 10 on your own tasks in the last 60 days?If not, Accelerated for now
  5. Four yeses: it may run Autonomous. Expect new work for checking it.
