The table

Pause and think

With saving on, what did the table need to know about each message to bring it back?

Reveal

Who said it, what was said, and when. One row per message, one column per fact. Everything a running app holds lives in memory, and memory dies when the app stops. Storage is a place outside the app that keeps rows after the app is gone. But a table is useless until you know the shape of what goes in it. That shape is what you model next.

01Question 1: what are the things?

Nouns are clues. The test is Create, Read, Update, Delete: can the app make one, find one, change one, remove one, on its own, and can there be many? If yes, it is a thing and gets its own table. If it only describes a thing, it is a fact. Tap each underlined word in Aarav's chat and say which.

Pause and think

Aarav picks a tone for each plan. Today, tone is a fact on the diagnosis. What would have to change in the product for tone to become a thing with its own table?

Reveal

If users could save named tones and reuse them across plans, a tone would be created, found, edited and deleted on its own. It would gain identity and a life, so it would become a thing. The word does not decide. What the product needs to do with it decides. That is the whole skill: drawing the line between a thing and a fact, for this app.

03Question 3: how do they connect?

A spreadsheet stores facts about one thing. A database stores facts and how things link. Every link has one of three shapes:

one-to-one

Each side has exactly oneRare. Worth a second look: it may be one table.

one-to-many

One parent, many childrenMost of every model. The many side carries a pointer back.

many-to-many

Both sides have manyNo single cell can hold "many". A third table does, one row per pairing.

Pick the shape for each pair in Aarav's app. When it is right, place the pointer: every row has an address called id (its key), and a link is just a column on the many side holding the other row's key. The convention is the other table's name, singular, plus _id.

04Rows or files?

Pause and think

Aarav attaches a screenshot of his Jira board. Can it go in a column?

Reveal

No. A cell holds a value you can type: text, a number, a date, yes or no. A screenshot is bytes. It goes in file storage, and the row keeps the link to it, plus the facts about it: which message it came with, its name, when it arrived. Rows for facts, files for everything large or binary, and a pointer between them.
