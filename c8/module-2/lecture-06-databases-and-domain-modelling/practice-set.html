03Your Whiteboard

The diagram from the lecture, drawn for your Assignment 1 app (Track B: Aarav's app). Use paper or any drawing app, with one line per box.

  • The user, the input, the output, and the process between them
  • UI: what runs on the user's machine
  • Backend: what must live on your server, and why (too big, private, or a key)
  • Every API call: address, action, key, package. The LLM call is one of them.
  • Storage: start with a file, and say where it lives
  • Inside the storage box: a question mark, for now
  • Beside the drawing: what you used to decide where each piece lives

The question mark

What is worth storing? Whatever your app's questions need, and nothing else. The lab shows you how to find out, using three questions: what are the things, what does each have, and how do they connect. Answer them for Aarav in sections 1 to 5 and for your app in section 7. Then replace the question mark with your tables.

05Rules

Write before you reveal.

Every "Pause and think" box in the lab needs a line from you before the reveal opens. Keep what you wrote, even when the reveal disagrees. That gap is where you learn.

Your questions decide what is worth storing.

Write your three questions before any table. A column that no question needs is a column you do not store.

Pointers on the many side. Always.

One cell cannot hold many. If you want to put a list in a cell, you have found a thing that needs its own table.

History is a row, not a cell.

If the product must remember what was true before, never overwrite the old value. Make it a row with a time on it.

No tool yet.

Do not open Supabase this week. The model is a paper exercise on purpose: if you cannot describe a table in words, you cannot build it in any tool.

LLM help: questions yes; the model no.

Ask a model to explain a shape you do not get. Do not paste a brief in and copy tables out. Placing the pointers yourself is the point.
