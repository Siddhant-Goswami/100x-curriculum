Lesson 4.9 · 9 of 9 in chapter 4 · about 3 min

Check yourself

Answer each one in your head, then open it.

How can a line through house sales price a house that was never sold?

The line holds the pattern, not the sales. Give it any size and it returns a price.

Why can a database say "not found" while a language model cannot?

A database looks a row up. A model calculates a next token from patterns, and there is always a next token.

Why start from bytes and not a dictionary of words?

Any text on a chip is already bytes, so every word, even a new one, can be spelled. Nothing is unknown.

What is the count table's one rule, and why does it lose "Paris"?

Open the row for the last token. The last token is "is", and that row mixes every sentence that ever had "is" in it.

Why not make the table look at the last ten tokens?

It would need more rows than grains of sand, and almost all would be empty: not found.

What do the gates between dials add?

They let the chain bend. Scaling alone, however many times, is still a straight line.

Why is each nudge small?

The same dials serve every sentence, so a big turn for one breaks many others.

Who wrote the answer key in this stage of training?

Nobody. The text did: the covered token is the answer.

Words you now own
Parameter (dial)
One learned number saying how much one signal pushes on another.
Token
A piece of text with a number. What a model reads and writes.
Hallucination
A fluent, confident, wrong guess, made because there is always a next token.
Byte pair encoding
Building tokens by merging the most common neighbouring pair, again and again.
Model
A wall of dials that turns tokens in into a chance for every next token.
Loss
The score for how far off a guess was. Lower is better.
Gradient
The direction each dial should turn to lower the loss.
Training
The loop: attempt, measure the mistake, feed it back.
Self-supervised learning
Learning where the text supplies its own answers.

In your build

  • Your database is the record. The model is the dials. Every AI app puts them side by side.
  • The hallucinated track list in Lecture 9 was a weak pattern with no "not found". Handing the model the record is what the reverse call in Lecture 10 does.
  • Tokens are what you are billed for and what the context limit counts.
  • The temperature setting in the Groq playground is the dice.
  • You cannot add a fact to the dials. You put it in the prompt, as in Lecture 10.
