The verifier is the job

From a failed project in 2023 to the three boxes that organise the next eight weeks. One story, four fixes that failed, two reports, the money, then a game that derived the module. Come back to sections 08 to 12 before you build anything.

Sat 19 Sep 2026Instructor: Siddhant GoswamiBoth tracks

01Where we stood: week 0, two of three done

  • 56 unique industries in the room. That is the design constraint: "what to build" cannot be taught as a lecture, only learned through exercises.
  • A hand raise: most had found an observation and run discovery. The plan was still to generate.

02One sentence from an editor, 2023

  • The client. A publicly traded media company, the world's third largest newspaper by distribution, tens of thousands of editors. December 2022, a month after ChatGPT.
  • The brief, fuzzy by design. Walk the floor, find what AI can do, build it, give us a number. Metric: time saved. Threshold: double-digit impact on the P&L.
  • Eight months. An internal Perplexity on GPT-3.5 for research, and an editing workflow that turned research into an exact 200-word column.
  • Result: 2%. The CEO said it was not significant. The editors said the sentence above.

03The room diagnoses it

  • Wrong metric (Vineet, Shubham). Work that neither saves cost nor raises revenue does not get paid for.
  • Incomplete problem statement (Purna, Satyaki). True, and normal. Completing it is the skill.
  • Real stakeholders, bottom-up adoption (Krishnan, Arjit Gupta). Both landed.

04Killing the four easy answers

  • A better model. SWE-bench Verified went from about 60% to near 100% in a year (Stanford HAI AI Index 2026), yet engineering hiring rose and FDE roles grew about 1,000%. A benchmark measures a task, not a job.
  • Better prompts. A prompt encodes an instruction. The editors could not state theirs: a good front page is "attention grabbing", "relatable". Ask a marketer of ten years what makes a good hook: "It should stop the scroll." Expertise settles into the subconscious.
  • More data. Month eight: fine-tune on years of published articles. It performed worse. Full fine-tuning rewrites the weights where old knowledge lived; a new behaviour costs an old one. That is catastrophic forgetting, covered in the fine-tuning lecture.
  • More time. Time without a standard produces more output at the same standard.

05What was actually missing

  • Only the editors knew what a good article looked like, and nobody had written it down. They lacked the vocabulary; the instructor, who had never published one, was the least qualified to write it.
  • So checking cost as much as writing. A 200 to 500 word article checked for mistakes and facts is, by the end, an article written.

The Verifier's Law: if you cannot write the verifier, you cannot build the system, and you cannot claim the capability.

That is why domain expertise is valuable in 2026: the expert is the only source of the verifier.

07Follow the money

  • The 6% who captured value built Claude Code, Cursor, Lovable, Codex. In every case engineers who knew what good code looks like had written it down, so the model could tell right from wrong and the loop ran fast. No secret sauce; a written verifier.
  • 4 May. Anthropic with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs forms a $1.5B services firm, later named Ode. Goldman: having the model alone does not change how you operate.
  • 11 May. OpenAI launches a deployment company, $4B+ from 19 investors, about 150 FDEs on day one. Its CTO: never a greater gap between what models can do and what enterprises use them for.
  • April to May. Google Cloud's Thomas Kurian: the era of the pilot is over.
  • September. Accenture and Google Cloud, up to 1,000 FDEs for Gemini Enterprise. The fifth such unit in under four months.

It is not a technology problem. Most experts have never written down how they judge, so every output routes through them.

08What a verifier looks like: Ogilvy in 15 criteria

  • A verifier is a written judgment of how you judge something. In 1959 David Ogilvy wrote the Rolls-Royce headline still called the best: At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock. In 1983 he wrote down every rule behind how he judged copy. That book is why Ogilvy & Mather runs without him.
  • Compressed into one prompt. For the first 100x website: score the copy out of 100 on 15 Ogilvy-inspired criteria (positioning, unique benefit, headline, reader focus, tone, simple language, evidence, emotion, story and more). A dead expert's judgment, still useful.
  • Then loop it. Give the verifier, set the threshold, and let the model score, improve and re-score until it gets there. That is an agent; designing the loop is loop engineering.

Do not ask the LLM to write your verifier. It returns the average of every expert, and the same slop anyone gets. Start with the task in your own job where you spend the most time judging.

09The verify game, round one: an app that adds two numbers

  • The rule of the game: write a criterion you could hand to a stranger or a model so they can verify the thing without you.
  • Know the process yourself. You cannot verify addition unless you know what it is. Expertise is the precondition.
  • Define the input (two numbers, not a cat and a dog) and the output (the sum, every time).
  • Write ideal input-output pairs. That set has a name: the golden dataset. Every ML engineer at the labs has built one.
  • Input, process, output will be asked in every lecture from here. Most blocked questions are blocked because one of the three is unclear.

11Round three: a million-dollar refund

  • The case. Should a Rolls-Royce customer get a refund? About 7.5 crore in India, close to a million dollars. The output is yes or no.
  • Varun's rule. Inputs: the car's cost X and the cost of issues within warranty Y. If Y exceeds X, refund. Same shape as the exam rule.
  • The counter. The comparison is trivial; getting the numbers is the work. Shipped as written, the rule can be gamed.
  • Judgment-based verification. Before the final output, ask a support executive who has done refunds for ten years and answers for mistakes. Same input, different output depending on whether the story smells right.
  • Judgment has two sources: a human, or an artificial one. Some transfers from human to model once written down, as Ogilvy's did. Some does not.

13From the Q&A

  • Why agents and Python, if a script would do? (Varun) For a fixed process with a fixed verifier, state the rule and let the LLM write the Python. Then: how do you verify the translation? Ambiguity becomes a bug, and unreliable things do not reach production. To verify the code you must read the language. That is the case against vibe coding: if an app costs no effort, a billion people can build it.
  • Deterministic MVP first? (Neha) Yes. You control everything in a deterministic system. Nobody, the labs included, controls a probabilistic output, only its distribution. One of the busiest teams at OpenAI builds no product and writes evals.
  • Does no-code skip the logic? (Nishant) No. Combined lectures carry the principles; Rahul's sessions carry the same logic. Code is paying upfront for control, like owning a car instead of renting.
  • Which tool for a second brain? (Devang) No tool recommendation, on principle. Bring the observation, the North Star and how success is measured; the tool follows.
  • Already built something? (Jagdeep) Run the exercise on it anyway: observation, hypothesis, what was built. Track switching (Arjit) goes to Rahul at the no-code orientation.

14What to do next

  1. Finish Assignment 1 step three. Generate the plan and publish your MVP tracker. Due Fri 9 Oct. No observation worth building? Look at what you live now: your job, your peers, this cohort's routine.
  2. Name your high-judgment task. Where you spend the most time judging rather than doing. One line: input, process, output.
  3. Write three golden pairs by hand. Three inputs with outputs you would accept, plus one you would reject and why. No LLM in this step.
  4. Run the stranger test. Hand the pairs and the rule to someone outside your field. If they cannot verify an output without asking you, it is not a verifier yet.

Keep this

The gap between what models can do and what businesses earn from them is not a capability gap. It is a verifier gap. If you cannot write down how you judge an output well enough that a stranger, or a model, could apply it, you cannot build a system for that task. Every lecture from here is one of three boxes: verifiable by a rule, by judgment, or not at all.
