Verify your own job

One real working week. Every atomic task. A verifier, or the reason there isn't one.

Follows Lecture 9, Fri 30 Oct 2026.

The brief

Log every atomic task from last week. For each, write how its output is verified and whether the data a machine would need exists. Then pick the one task you will build a model for. Copy the template.

Why this exists

  • Aarav was a proxy. Now draw the rule-versus-judgment line on work you are paid for. Notes, section 10.
  • It feeds the rest of Module 2. Your candidate task becomes your data, model and evaluation.
  • Verifiable is not automated. A slow verifier or missing data keeps a task human for years.

A verifier that only answers in a quarter or longer is an outcome signal: record it, but type the row none. A model cannot learn from feedback that arrives months later, mixed with everything else that moved.

03Track B: the job you want 0 of 4 ready

Not working? Reconstruct one real person's week. Weaker on purpose: you are verifying someone else's work.

04Rules

  1. Predict before you count. The gap is part of the result.
  2. Your week, not a generic one. An LLM listing your role's tasks gives the average job, which labs already buy data for. It may cluster your exported calendar and Slack, phrase a verifier, or second-guess a bucket.
  3. "It's subjective" is not a verifier. Name a judge, a signal and a time, or type the row none and say what is missing.
  4. Bucket on verifier, data and cost, not type alone.
    • Automate: a check catches mistakes without a person, the data exists, a miss is cheap.
    • Augment: a machine drafts and a person signs off, because the check needs judgment or a miss is expensive.
    • Human: no verifier yet, only an outcome signal on a costly decision, or human by nature.
  5. Verified by rule is not produced by rule. If a script or template can produce a rule row, no LLM. SQL from a vague question still needs one to write it; a rule checks it.
  6. Model work stays locked until the sheet is in, except section 07. Company data never goes into a hosted chatbot; confirm you may use it on your own machine.

His candidate task

"Why did metric X move?" Slack asks. Needs an LLM, judged within two days, about 140 past examples, roughly 6 hours a week, and his manager approved using the channel export and aggregated tables locally. The SQL row was safer to evaluate; he chose the asks because they take twice the time and sit closer to his North Star.

Line test on that task

  1. Turn the ask into metric, region, period and comparison. Orange, once asks come through a Slack form.
  2. Pull the metric and its baseline with SQL. Orange.
  3. Break the change down by SKU, courier, pincode and channel. Orange: arithmetic.
  4. Pick the driver and name the likely cause. Purple: "62% of the extra returns are one SKU" is arithmetic; "because the size chart changed" comes from patterns in past cases.
  5. Write the reply. Purple: it depends on who reads it and what they can act on.
  6. Check every number in the reply against step 3. Orange.

The model only has to do steps 4 and 5.

07Optional stretch: your first baseline

45 minutes, to see how a small open model does on your task before you build anything.

  1. Install Qwen 3.5 on Ollama (about 7 GB, runs on a 16 GB laptop). Local only; anonymize anything sensitive.
  2. Give it the input from three golden pairs, with no extra instructions.
  3. Mark each output: template (right shape?) and data (right facts?).
  4. One line on what it got wrong and what it was missing.

Right template, wrong data is the hallucination from Notes, section 04. Aarav's run gave a tidy reply blaming courier delays; it never saw the size-chart change. These scores are the baseline your model must beat in week four.

09After you count

Stuck? Write the best verifier you can and flag the row; never leave it blank. Bring flagged rows to the next live session or office hours. For a second opinion on a bucket, paste your tasks into the delegation framework. It sorts your list; it does not write it.

Warm-up answers

  • A. Not atomic. Split it into outputs someone checks, like "weekly sell-through report for the category team".
  • B. No judge or signal. Try: finance controller · signs off the reconciliation · same day.
  • C. Wrong type. Dashboard totals reconcile to the source: a rule. "Manager reviews it" is not the verifier.
  • D. Too small. Fold it into the weekly business review row.
