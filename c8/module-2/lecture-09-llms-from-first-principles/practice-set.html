Verify your own job

One real working week. Every atomic task. A verifier, or the reason there isn't one.

Follows Lecture 9, Fri 30 Oct 2026.

The brief

Log every atomic task from your last full working week. For each: how its output is verified, how fast that check answers, and whether the data a machine would need exists. Then pick the one task you will build a model for.

Why this exists

  • Aarav was a proxy. The skill is drawing the rule-versus-judgment line on work you are paid for. Notes, section 10.
  • It is the input to the rest of Module 2. Your candidate task becomes your data, model and evaluation.
  • Verifiable is not automated. A slow verifier or missing data keeps a task human for years. This sheet measures both.

A verifier that answers in a quarter or longer is an outcome signal. Record it, but it does not count toward your third number: a model cannot learn from feedback that arrives months later, mixed with everything else that moved.

03Track B: the job you want 0 of 4 ready

Not working right now? Reconstruct one real person's week. It is the weaker version on purpose: you are verifying someone else's work.

For deliverables 3 and 4, use the artifact you collected or a public equivalent (a public dataset, a published report). Say which.

04Rules

  1. Predict before you count. The gap is part of the result.
  2. Your week, not a generic one. Do not ask an LLM to list your role's tasks; that gives the average job, which labs can already buy data for. An LLM may cluster your exported calendar and Slack into rows, phrase a verifier, or second-guess a bucket.
  3. "It's subjective" is not a verifier. Every judgment row names a judge, a signal and a time. Can't? Type is none; say what is missing.
  4. Bucket on verifier, data and cost, not type alone.
    • Automate: a check catches mistakes without a person, the data exists, a miss is cheap.
    • Augment: a machine drafts, a person signs off, because the check needs judgment or a miss is expensive.
    • Human: no verifier yet, or only an outcome signal on a high-cost decision, or human by nature.
  5. Verified by rule is not produced by rule. For each rule row, note whether a script or template can produce it. If yes, no LLM. SQL from a vague question still needs an LLM to write, while a rule checks it.
  6. Model work stays locked until the sheet is in, except section 07. Company data never goes into a hosted chatbot; confirm you may use your candidate task's data on your own machine.

His candidate task

"Why did metric X move?" Slack asks. Needs an LLM to produce, verifier answers within two days, about 140 past examples, roughly 6 hours a week, and his manager approved using the channel export and aggregated tables locally. The SQL row was the safer pick (a rule makes it easy to evaluate); he chose the asks because they cost twice the time and sit closer to his North Star.

Line test on that task

  1. Turn the ask into metric, region, period and comparison. Orange, once asks come through a Slack form instead of free text.
  2. Pull the metric and its baseline with SQL. Orange.
  3. Break the change down by SKU, courier, pincode and channel. Orange: it is arithmetic.
  4. Pick the driver that explains it and name the likely cause. Purple: "one SKU is 62% of the extra returns" is arithmetic, but "because the size chart changed" comes from patterns in past cases.
  5. Write the reply for the category manager. Purple: what to say depends on who reads it and what they can act on.
  6. Check every number in the reply against step 3. Orange.

Four of six steps are orange. The model only has to do steps 4 and 5.

07Optional stretch: your first baseline

45 minutes. See how a small open model does on your own task before you build anything.

  1. Install Qwen 3.5 on Ollama. It needs about 7 GB and runs on a 16 GB laptop. Local only: no hosted models, and anonymize anything sensitive.
  2. Give it the input from three of your golden pairs, with no extra instructions.
  3. For each output, mark two things: template (right shape?) and data (right facts?).
  4. Write one line on what it got wrong and what it was missing to get it right.

Right template, wrong data is the hallucination from Notes, section 04, on your own work. Aarav's run produced a tidy root-cause reply that blamed courier delays; it never saw the size-chart change. These three scores are the baseline your model must beat in week four.

09After you count

When you are stuck, write the best verifier you can and flag the row. Never leave it blank. Flagged rows are good questions for the next live session or office hours. For a second opinion on a bucket, paste your own tasks into the delegation framework. It sorts your list; it does not write it.

His prediction was off by 35 points. The 85% to 54% gap is work a machine cannot learn yet, for lack of data or a fast verifier. That gap, and the hours no rule covers, are where his time should shift.

The cohort benchmark: across three years of 100x alumni jobs, about 80% of tasks were verifiable. Compare it with your second number, not your third. After submissions close, the next live session shows the cohort's numbers by role and the most common surprising rows.

What happens next: your candidate task carries the rest of Module 2: data, then a model, then an evaluation against your ten golden pairs. "Help the team" gets you four weeks of rewriting this sheet.

Warm-up answers

  • A. Not atomic. Split it into outputs someone checks, like "weekly sell-through report for the category team".
  • B. No judge or signal. Try: finance controller · signs off the reconciliation · same day.
  • C. Wrong type. Dashboard totals reconcile to the source, so it is a rule, and "manager reviews it" is not the verifier.
  • D. Too small. Fold it into the weekly business review row it belongs to.
  • E. An outcome signal only, a year late. Add a fast judge: manager signs off the assumptions and sources that week.
