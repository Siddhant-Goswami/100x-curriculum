Practice set 1 · Lecture 9 · Both tracks

Verify your own job

One real working week. Every atomic task. A verifier, or the reason there isn't one.

Follows Lecture 9, Fri 30 Oct 2026.

The brief

Write your North Star. List every atomic task from last week. For each, write its success criteria, whether it moves your North Star, its verifier and verification type, and whether the data exists. Copy the template: Aarav's week is the first tab, yours is the second.

01Start with your North Star

One sentence, six months out: By [month + year], I am [role or identity] doing [specific thing] at [level, income or scale]. Someone outside the cohort should be able to check it on the date. Week 0 notes, sections 02 and 03.

It decides what is worth verifying. An atomic task that does not move your North Star gets one line and nothing more.

02Fill the sheet 0 of 5 ready

The three shares at the top of the sheet fill themselves. Across 100x alumni, about 80% of tasks were verifiable.

Not working right now? Interview one person in the role you want about one specific day ("Open your calendar for last Tuesday: what did you produce, and who checked it?") and fill the sheet for their week.

03Rules

  • Your week, from your records. An LLM listing your role's tasks gives you the average job, not yours.
  • Bucket on the verifier and the data, not the verification type alone. Automate when a rule catches mistakes and the data exists. Augment when a judge has to sign off. Human when it is unverifiable.
  • If a script or formula can produce it, no LLM.
  • Company data stays on your machine. No model work on these tasks until the sheet is in.

04What to submit 0 of 2 ready

Stuck on a row? Write your best guess and move on; bring it to office hours. For a second opinion on a bucket, paste your tasks into the delegation framework.

05What happens next

The next practice set picks one task from this sheet and builds a model for it, so a sharp verifier and real data matter. "Help the team" gets you four weeks of rewriting this sheet. Verifier first, data second: notes, section 10.
