Eval Co-pilot

Catch the case a friend’s AI feature gets wrong, before they ship it.

Every classmate just shipped an AI app: paste a workflow, get a plan. Ask one if it is good. They’ll say it works on what they tried. They picked what they tried. Take ten real inputs, run every one, and check each answer against what they said good looks like. One fails in a way they didn’t know about.

Teaches How to verify the verifier.
You win whenThey change something, re-run, and the failing case passes.Write your bar before you build, for example: at least one failure they didn’t know about, fixed and re-run within the session.
Test with a classmate

A classmate with a live AI app, the one from Lecture 8 counts.

And a stranger

A cohort-mate you have never worked with, or anyone outside the cohort. They have never seen your app and get no help from you.

Five moves, on this problem

Each one is a lecture from the last four weeks.

DiscoveryDo it by hand

Green by Sun 3 PM
  • Sit with one classmate and their live app. You are the Eval Co-pilot.
  • With them, write ten golden pairs from real inputs: what their user types, and what a good answer has in it.
  • Run all ten by hand. Mark each answer pass or fail against their verifier.
  • Show them the fail they didn’t expect. Do they change something and run it again?

Sit with it. Which fail surprised them, and which did they already know about?

Draw the line

Draw yours on paper. This is where to start, not the answer.

Code decides

Same input, same answer, every time.

  • Sending each input to their URL
  • A 500 or a timeout is a fail
  • Rule checks: has a number, has a baseline, under 300 words
  • The pass count, and run 1 against run 2
Your callWho judges the checks a rule can’t?

AI decides

Judgment. Can differ run to run.

  • Checks a rule can’t read: “names the real bottleneck”
  • Why an answer failed, in one line
  • No-goAn AI judge nobody checked against pairs you graded by hand.

A judge that says “looks good” to everything is worse than no judge: it manufactures confidence. Grade three pairs by hand first. If your AI judge disagrees with you on those, it doesn’t judge.

What is worth storing

The questions it must answer tomorrow. Draw the tables that answer them.

  • Which cases failed in run 1 and passed in run 2?
  • Which check fails most often?
  • What exactly did their app say each time?
The one link that is your proofresult→golden_pair

Each result points at its golden pair and its run. That is how one case, run twice, shows the fix worked.

  • Their key never reaches you. Store their public URL, nothing else of theirs.
  • History is a row. A new run never overwrites the last one.

Watch them use it

What they did, not what they said.

Counts

  • They changed a prompt, a model or code
  • The re-run, with the case now passing
  • A failure they called new

Doesn’t count

  • “Good tool”
  • A score with no failed answer shown
  • Cases you wrote without them

Traps

  • A leaderboard. One score per app says nothing about their own inputs.
  • The rubber stamp. A judge that passes everything.
  • Their hand-picked cases. They picked them because they pass.
  • An unchecked AI judge. Calibrate it on pairs you already graded.
