BillShield

Turn a bill you don’t trust into one message worth sending.

Ask a classmate for a bill they paid without reading: phone, broadband, electricity, a card statement. Go through it line by line together. Somewhere is a charge they can’t explain. A ₹199 pack they never asked for. A late fee on a bill paid on time. They noticed. They never wrote in, because writing that message is a chore.

Teaches Where code must decide, because a model can’t be trusted to add.
You win whenThey send the message.Write your bar before you build, for example: sent with fewer than 20 words changed. If every line checks out, saying so, with the maths, is a real answer.
Test with a classmate

A classmate with a real bill from the last three months.

And a stranger

A cohort-mate you have never worked with, or anyone outside the cohort. They have never seen your app and get no help from you.

Draw the line

Draw yours on paper. This is where to start, not the answer.

Code decides

Same input, same answer, every time.

  • Totals, taxes and the difference from the promise
  • Charges that repeat across months
  • Dates: billing period, due date, late fees
  • What gets masked before saving
Your callIs a charge wrong, or only unexplained?

AI decides

Judgment. Can differ run to run.

  • Reading the photo into lines
  • Each line in plain words
  • The message, in their tone
  • No-goAI states an amount code didn’t compute.

The model will happily add up the bill for you. It will also be confidently wrong by ₹40. Every number in the message comes from code.

What is worth storing

The questions it must answer tomorrow. Draw the tables that answer them.

  • Has this charge shown up on my earlier bills?
  • Which messages did I send, and about which line?
  • What was I promised, and when did it change?
The one link that is your proofmessage→line_item

The message points at the line it disputes, with when it was sent and how many words were changed. Without it you can’t show the tool did anything.

  • Rows for facts, files for bytes. The bill image is a file. Its row holds the link.
  • Store the least. No account numbers, phone numbers or addresses.
  • Login and row-level security. Two accounts, one cross-read, an empty result.

Watch them use it

What they did, not what they said.

Counts

  • They sent it to the company
  • Words changed before sending, counted
  • A charge they found that you didn’t

Doesn’t count

  • “This is useful”
  • A sample bill from the internet
  • A message they’d send “later”

Traps

  • A bill summariser. Explaining the bill isn’t the job. The message is.
  • The model doing maths. Every rupee comes from code.
  • Inventing an error. “Every line checks out” is an answer.
  • One person’s bill on another’s screen. That is what the two-user test is for.
