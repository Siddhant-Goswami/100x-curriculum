Concept Check

Find where a learner stops understanding and starts reciting.

Ask a classmate why the API key can’t live in the browser. They start well. By the third sentence they’re saying “for security reasons”. The words keep coming. The thinking has stopped. Point at that sentence, ask one question about what breaks, and see if they can work it out.

Teaches How to judge a judgment about someone’s mind.

Test with a classmate

A classmate who says they get it.

And a stranger

A cohort-mate you have never worked with, or anyone outside the cohort. They have never seen your app and get no help from you.

DiscoveryDo it by hand

Green by Sun 3 PM
  • Sit with two classmates who say they get one of the ideas below. You are Concept Check.
  • Ask them to work it out, not define it. Push with why, and what breaks without it.
  • Mark the sentence where it turned into a phrase. Ask one follow-up.
  • Then give a new case they haven’t seen. Each case, with its known answer, is a golden pair. Write ten.

Ideas to test. Why does a backend exist at all? · What does one API call carry? · Why can’t the key live in the browser? · Why does the app forget without storage? · Why does the pointer sit on the many side? · Why is history a row, not a cell? · How do you decide where a piece lives?

Sit with it. Did both break at the same sentence? That is worth a lot.

AI decides

Judgment. Can differ run to run.

  • Finding the recited sentence
  • Writing the one follow-up
  • No-goThe model decides on its own that they understand now.

A model asked “do they understand now?” says yes, kindly, to everyone. The new case has an answer you wrote before the session. That answer is the judge, not the model’s mood.

What is worth storing

The questions it must answer tomorrow. Draw the tables that answer them.

  • Which idea does this learner keep reciting?
  • Did the second try pass a new case?

The one link that is your proofretry→gap

The second try points at the gap it answers. Without it you can’t show a gap was named and then closed.

  • Consent first. Recording a classmate thinking out loud needs a yes.
  • Store the least. Their explanation, not their name in the shared view.
  • Login and row-level security. Two accounts, one cross-read, an empty result.

Watch them use it

What they did, not what they said.

Counts

  • Failed a new case before, passed a different one after
  • The recited sentence, quoted
  • A follow-up that didn’t work, and what you tried next

Doesn’t count

  • “That was helpful”
  • The same phrase, said faster
  • A case they’d already seen

Traps

  • A quiz. Picking from four options tests recognition. You are testing the opposite.
  • A soft judge. The model passes everyone.
  • Ideas they never met. Stick to Module 2.
  • A follow-up that gives the answer. Ask what breaks. Don’t tell them.
