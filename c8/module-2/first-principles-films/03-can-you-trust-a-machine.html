Can you trust a machine?

The screen says "saved". One second later the power dies. Is it saved? Then: a second kind of memory, one that guesses.

How to play: click the raised pieces in each drawing, or the buttons under it. Reach the goal and the explanation opens.

01Break one upgrade

A user upgrades to a paid plan. That is two writes: take the credits, raise the plan.

What you just found

Accountants never edit the books directly. They add one line to a journal, then change the books. Rows are spread across the disk, so a power cut mid-edit leaves half of them changed and nobody can tell which. A journal line is either whole or visibly torn. That journal is the write-ahead log.

Principle FourRecord the change in one place before changing anything else.

The crack you found is one of four. The heart of a database is what it promises when things fail:

What goes wrongThe promiseCalled
Power dies between the two writesAll or nothingAtomicity
A typo writes pr, not proNo rule is ever brokenConsistency
Two people save the same row; the second wins silentlyNo one overwrites anyoneIsolation
"Saved" was still in the leaky bucketsDone means storedDurability

The screen showing a message is not the message being safe. The tick is.

03Copies, and one original

Forty thousand teams log in and one machine cannot keep up. So add a second. Every write goes to the leader, which sends its journal to a follower that replays it.

What you just found

  • Floors. To grow further, split: teams A to F on one machine, G to M on the next. Each floor keeps its own copies.
  • When the cable is cut between two copies there are two choices: show what this copy last heard, or make the customer wait. There is no third.
  • The warehouse. One more copy, arranged for reports, on its own machine.

Follower, index, warehouse. When two disagree, which is right? Decide in advance: keep one original, the system of record.

Principle FiveKeep one original. Every copy can be thrown away and remade.

04A memory that guesses

Every memory so far is exact. Ask a table of sales for a house that was never sold and you get not found. An estate agent could still guess, from patterns in past sales. Draw the pattern as a line. A line is two numbers: how steep, and where it starts.

What you just found

Those two numbers are parameters. Start them random. Guess one house, check the real sale, nudge the numbers a little. A million times. Then throw the sales away, and the line still prices a house nobody sold.

Everything in a language model is this line, scaled up.

05One job, repeated

For language, text is chopped into pieces and each piece gets a number: un | believ | able is 403 | 31141 | 481. A piece is a token. Inside the model, one number says how hard "France" pushes toward "Paris". It is not a switch. It is a dial, the same kind of thing as "how steep".

The model does one job: score every possible next token, pick one, add it to the end, go again.

What you just found

The first principle of a language modelEverything it writes is the next token, guessed from patterns held in its dials.

A table has "not found". A model does not. Where the pattern is weak, the guess is whatever sounds right: fluent, confident, wrong. That is hallucination.

"I don't know" is also a pattern: a learned response, not a check. So when it matters, hand the model the record and make it read before it writes.

06In your build

  • Your database is the record. The model is the dials. Every AI app puts them side by side.
  • The hallucinated track list in Lecture 9 was a weak pattern with no "not found".
  • Handing the model the record is what the reverse call in Lecture 10 does.

07Check yourself

Answer in your head, then open it.

Why is one journal line safer than editing the rows directly?

It is written in one place, so after a crash it is either whole or visibly torn, and the machine can finish what it says.

A message appears on your screen. Is it safe?

Not yet. Safe is when the server has written its journal line to disk and said so: the tick.

The network between two copies is cut. What are the only two choices?

Show what this copy last heard, which may be old, or make the customer wait.

Why can a database say "not found" while a language model cannot?

A database looks a row up. A model calculates a next token from patterns, and there is always a next token.

How can a line through house sales price a house that was never sold?

The line holds the pattern, not the sales. Give it any size and it returns a price.
