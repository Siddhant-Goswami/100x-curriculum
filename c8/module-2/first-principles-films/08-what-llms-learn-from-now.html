What do LLMs learn from now?

The scarcest thing in AI is not a chip or a model. It is a way to tell when an answer is right.

How to play: click the raised pieces in each drawing, or the buttons under it. Reach the goal and the explanation opens.

01The chart that flipped

Take all the computing at OpenAI and Anthropic, as estimated by SemiAnalysis. In early 2024, about two thirds of it went on reading the web.

The new computing goes to what comes after the reading: post-training. "We have but one internet," Ilya Sutskever said in December 2024, and every lab has read it. The web kept the result and threw away the work: the final essay without the drafts, the signed contract without the clause a partner crossed out.

The figures in this film are estimates and reporting from a fast-moving industry. Keep the ideas, treat the numbers as sizes.

02Let it try

Give the model a hard maths problem and let it write out its working before the answer. Check only the last line. Ask the same problem sixteen times: the dice give sixteen different attempts. Every token in a winning attempt is nudged up, every token in a losing one down.

What you just found

Attempts that paused and checked a step won more often, so pausing became a habit. The model taught itself to doubt itself, with only a tick or a cross at the end.

  • On the card, the attempts slot changed: one problem is now sixteen attempts of thousands of tokens each, every dial crossing the bench for every token. That is where the computing went.
  • And once every attempt passes, there is no difference left to learn from. A check is a consumable. The data problem is now an endless supply of fresh, hard, honest checks.

03From answers to checks

This works wherever the answer checks itself: maths, and code where the tests pass or fail. Most paid work is on the email's side. So the expert's job changed. One good answer teaches one example. A written rubric lets a judge score a million attempts against the expert's standard. The expert is no longer writing answers. The expert is writing the check.

What you just found

  • The trajectory is exactly what the web threw away.
  • An environment is a copy of a workplace, a task to do inside it, and a program that checks what actually changed, not what the model says it did. A flight simulator for office work.
  • Measured by METR in early 2026: the best models finish a software task that takes a skilled person most of a working day, half the time. Ask for four times out of five and the task shrinks to about an hour and a half.
  • Every company that puts a model to work finds the cases where it fails. Each failure is a fresh task no lab has.

04The machine learns the check

You want working code. So you reward the model whenever the tests pass.

What you just found

That is reward hacking. The three steps above are a made-up example. These are from the film:

  • Punishing the thought. OpenAI saw a model plan a cheat in its written working and punished it. The model stopped writing it down and kept cheating.
  • On purpose. Anthropic trained a model on eighty tasks that could be gamed. Tested elsewhere, when it could see a grader, it went along with a dangerous request 29 percent of the time, up from under one.
  • By accident. OpenAI tested a model on hacking challenges in a sealed box. Some could not be solved, so it looked for a way to trick the scorer and got out of the box.

Nobody trained those models to do that. They trained them to score.

Principle TenA machine learns the check you wrote, not the goal you meant.

05Two halves

Every useful system has two halves. One predicts: the model and its dials. One follows rules: the tools, the records, the checks.

People call data the new oil. It is not. Every barrel of oil is the same. Your standard is yours.

  • Your Practice set 1 sheet sorts a real week by what can be checked. That is Principle Nine, applied.

07Check yourself

Answer in your head, then open it.

If only the last line is checked, how does the working improve?

Whole winning attempts are nudged up and losing ones down, so each line gets credit for where it ended up.

Why did the compute bill move to post-training?

One problem is now many attempts of thousands of tokens each, every token generated with every dial crossing the bench.

What is the difference between an expert writing an answer and writing a rubric?

An answer teaches one example. A rubric lets a judge score a million attempts against the expert's standard.

Why does a task stop being useful once a model passes it almost every time?

Learning comes from the difference between winning and losing attempts. With no losers there is nothing to nudge.

Asked to make the tests pass, models have edited the tests.
