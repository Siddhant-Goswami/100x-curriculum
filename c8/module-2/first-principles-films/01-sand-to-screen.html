Film 1 of 8 · How Computers Work: From Sand to Screen · 3:18

How does sand do maths?

Melt sand, grow a crystal, carve it with light. After that it can add. Start with the smallest piece and build up.

How to play: click the raised pieces in each drawing, or the buttons under it. Reach the goal and the explanation opens.

01A gate on a wire

Sand is mostly silicon. Push electrons down a wire and that flow is electricity. Carve silicon the right way and you get a gate on that wire: a little voltage opens it.

What you just found

That gate is a transistor. It is opened by electricity, and what comes out is electricity, so one transistor can open another, in chains as long as you like. A phone chip holds tens of billions of them.

02Ones and zeros

A gate has two states. Call on a 1 and off a 0. To count higher, line switches up and give each one a value.

What you just found

Each switch you add doubles how far you can count. Writing numbers this way is binary. And anything can become a number: a letter (A is 65, because we agreed), a pixel (three numbers: red, green, blue), a sound (a long list tracing a wave). Everything on a screen is numbers, and every number is switches.

03Two switches in a row

Now wire two switches one after the other on the same wire.

What you just found

Current gets through only when both are on. Now look at what adding two digits needs:

A + BIn binaryCarry digit
0 + 0000
0 + 1010
1 + 0010
1 + 1101

The carry is 1 only when both are 1. That is the circuit you just tested. One more small circuit gives the other digit, and you have an adder.

04Be the adder

Before the circuit does it, do it by hand. The answer switches are worth 4, 2 and 1.

What you just found

In the chip, nobody does the maths. Switches open and shut, and the answer is where the current ends up. Subtracting is adding a negative number. Multiplying is adding again and again. Pack these circuits together and you have the calculator at the heart of every chip.

05A loop that remembers

Wire two gates in a loop, so each one's output feeds the other's gate.

What you just found

That loop is one bit of memory. A program is a list of instructions stored as ones and zeros in that same memory. A clock ticks billions of times a second, and on each tick the chip does the next instruction.

The first principleAtoms make electricity. Electricity flips transistors. Transistors speak in ones and zeros.

06In your build

Every row you saved in Lecture 7 and every token a model sends back is this gate, flipped in the right order. The next seven films only stack layers on top of it.

07Check yourself

Answer in your head, then open it.

Why does it matter that a transistor is opened by electricity?

Because its output is also electricity, so one transistor can open the next. That is what lets them form circuits.

With switches worth 4, 2 and 1, which number is 101? How many numbers can three switches show?

4 + 1 = 5. Three switches show eight numbers, 0 to 7.

Two switches in a row let current through only when both are on. Which digit of an addition is that?

The carry digit.

How does a loop of two transistors keep a 1 after the input is removed?

Each one's output holds the other one's gate open, so the loop keeps itself on.

Words you now own

Binary
Writing numbers with only 1 (on) and 0 (off).
Adder
A small circuit of switches whose output is the sum of its inputs.
Bit
Two transistors in a loop, holding a 1 or a 0.
Program
A list of instructions stored as ones and zeros.
Clock
The tick on which the chip does its next instruction.
