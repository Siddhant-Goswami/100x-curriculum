What are attention and transformers?

The count table only saw the last token. The dials read all five and know that "capital" and "France" matter. How?

How to play: click the raised pieces in each drawing, or the buttons under it. Reach the goal and the explanation opens.

01A map of words

A token's number is only a code. 4881 for France says nothing about 6342 for Paris. So rate every token on rulers instead. With two rulers, every token is a spot on a map.

What you just found

  • Nobody writes the ratings. Each one is a dial. If Paris and London sit close, one sentence ("flew to Paris") teaches both, and the loss is lower. The mistakes drew the map.
  • A real map has thousands of rulers, about twelve thousand in GPT-3. So keep the list of numbers: a vector. A token's list is its embedding.

02One word, two meanings

Each token gets its spot alone, before it sees its neighbours. That breaks on a word like "bank".

What you just found

"You shall know a word by the company it keeps," a linguist wrote in 1957. So each token needs a way to look at the others and move its spot.

One fix first: the map has no sense of order, so each token also gets a position stamp for its place in line.

03Every token asks a question

Every token makes three things from its list of numbers: a question (what it is looking for), a label (what it offers) and a message (what it passes on if chosen). Follow the last token, "is".

What you just found

  1. Ask. "is" asks: what am I describing? Every earlier token holds up its label.
  2. Match. Line the question up with a label, multiply pair by pair, add.
  3. Share. Turn the totals into shares that add up to 100.
  4. Collect. Take each token's message at that volume, and move to a new spot.

It is no longer just "is". It is "is, after the capital of France". That is attention. Engineers call the three things query, key and value. A token reads only the tokens before it, and every token does this at once.

04Listen, then think

After listening, each token thinks on its own: its list of numbers runs through a big block of dials and a new list comes out. Listen, then think. That pair is one layer.

What you just found

  • Heads. One question is not enough, so the model asks many side by side. GPT-3 runs 96.
  • Stack it. GPT-3 stacks 96 layers. Lower ones tend to sort out grammar, higher ones meaning.
  • That stack is a transformer, the T in GPT.

Nobody wrote a rule that says "look at France". Listening to France lowered the loss.

Principle EightMeaning comes from company. Every token listens to the ones before it, and becomes what it hears.

05The bill

All this listening has a cost. Every token listens to every token before it.

What you just found

And for every token that comes out, every dial is read again: billions of numbers carried from memory to the chip to produce one token. The chip can do the maths faster than memory can feed it. That is the next film.

06In your build

  • The model can only listen to what is in the prompt. If "capital" and "France" are not there, nothing can attend to them.
  • Long prompts cost more than their length suggests.
  • Embeddings are what search by meaning runs on. You will use them in Lecture 12.

07Check yourself

Answer in your head, then open it.

What is the difference between a token's code and its embedding?

The code is an agreed or counted number that says nothing about meaning. The embedding is a list of numbers found by training.

Why do "Paris" and "London" drift together if nobody rates the words?

When they sit close, one sentence teaches both, which lowers the loss. Lower loss is the only direction nudges push.

What problem does "bank" expose, and what solves it?

A token gets its spot alone, before seeing its neighbours. Attention lets it move based on the words around it.

Why can a token not look at the tokens after it?

They are the answer it is being trained to guess.

Why does doubling a text roughly quadruple the listening?

Every token listens to every earlier one, so the work grows with the number of pairs.
