What hardware powers LLMs?

What does the chip spend its time on, and what does a model cost to make?

How to play: click the raised pieces in each drawing, or the buttons under it. Reach the goal and the explanation opens.

01The right shape of chip

One dial multiplies the number coming in and adds the result to a running total. Listening, thinking, the chances at the top: all of it is multiply and add. Two steps for every dial, for every token.

What you just found

A normal processor is a few brilliant workers who can do anything, one step after another. A GPU is thousands of simple workers doing the same step at once. It was built to colour millions of pixels for video games. A language model needs exactly that.

A top GPU does about a thousand trillion steps a second, so the maths for one token of an 8-billion-dial model takes 16 millionths of a second. Almost free.

02What does the chip wait for?

But the dials do not live inside the workers. They sit on the GPU's own workbench, the leaky buckets from film 2.

What you just found

For every token, every dial travels from the bench to the workers. Eight billion dials at 16 bits each is 16 gigabytes, carried across for one token. The workers spend almost all their time waiting.

The lessonThe maths is cheap. Moving the numbers is the cost.

03Make every trip count

Every trick in this machine is about carrying numbers fewer times, or carrying fewer of them.

What you just found

TrickWhat it doesWhat it costs
BatchingOne trip of the dials serves hundreds of people's tokensYou need many users at once
QuantizationHolds each dial in 8 bits, not 16Slightly rougher dials
KV cacheKeeps earlier tokens' labels and messages instead of remaking themMemory that grows with the chat

The KV cache is Principle Three again: faster reading means a second copy. It is one reason chats have a length limit.

04Know before you spend

Answering is the cheap part. Training is the bill: 6 steps per dial, times 400 billion dials, times 15 trillion tokens. Meta trained Llama 3 on 16,000 GPUs for months. Tens to hundreds of millions of dollars. You do not get to try it twice.

What you just found

Engineers call these scaling laws. The line also says how to spend: grow the dials and the text together, about twenty tokens for every dial.

05In your build

  • Long chats get slower and hit a limit because the cache grows with every token.
  • A model that runs on a laptop is usually a quantized one.
  • You rent these trips by the token. Shorter prompts are cheaper trips.

06Check yourself

Answer in your head, then open it.

For an 8-billion-dial model, which takes longer: the maths or moving the dials?

Moving the dials, by about three hundred times: 5 thousandths of a second against 16 millionths.

Why is a GPU a better shape than a CPU for this work?

The work is billions of copies of one small step, and a GPU has thousands of simple workers doing that step at once.

Which earlier principle is the KV cache an example of, and what does it cost?

Principle Three. The copy takes memory that grows with the chat.

How can a lab predict a model's quality before training it?

Train small models, plot their loss against compute, and extend the line.
