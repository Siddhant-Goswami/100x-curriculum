Building UI: design to code

From Aarav's Friday status report to four lines of Gradio, by way of a vote that lost. Read sections 02 to 09 once, slowly, with your own observation open beside them. Come back to 11 to 13 when you sit down to install and run.

Sat 26 Sep 2026Instructor: Siddhant GoswamiCode track

01The room check: who had done week 0

The prerequisite was one observation, one hypothesis and one conversation with a real user. Two framing points before any design. You are not married to one observation: until the Assignment 1 deadline (9 October), the more observations you try and people you speak to, the better you get at testing assumptions. And when building becomes a commodity, finding the right users and solving their problem is the part AI cannot yet do.

02Start from one person, not a persona

  • The observation. Ambitious early career professionals in urban India arrive AI-aware and motivated, but fail at selection, scoping and first proof. They see AI at the category level and cannot turn it into one grounded workflow. It contains no solution: an observation reports what you saw, and stops.
  • The hypothesis. They are stuck because they lack a method to go from broad AI possibility to one scoped workflow. Test: a workflow diagnosis system plus a guided build sprint, and 60% of active students identify one user, map one repeated workflow, build an AI-assisted version and demo it within 90 days. At 10 people, 6 of 10.
  • The move. Do not solve for a category. Solve for one named person.

03The cheapest test is three boxes on a whiteboard

The hypothesis names two solutions. Take only part one, the diagnosis, and find the cheapest way to test it with Aarav. The input came from a message to him: what is the one thing you repeat every week, in 3 to 4 lines? His reply, compressed, is the input. The output is a plan for automating part of it.

The success test is not whether Aarav likes the plan. It is whether following it saves him time, say 1 hour a week. If it saves nothing, the plan failed. Designing meant understanding what the user needs and the minimum solution that tests it. It does not mean opening Figma.

04Prateek's observation, and the stranger test

Prateek's: project knowledge exists, but access depends on personal memory and network, so under deadline people reuse the precedent they remember rather than the best fit.

  • Observation: too generic. "Organization" does not say finance, healthcare or education, and an organization is not a person. Name the one person who hits this most often, with their designation, and write from their point of view.
  • Hypothesis: no number. "Noticeably faster" cannot be falsified. Add a measurement and a stop rule, or you keep spending time with no signal for when to pivot.
  • Input and output: good. Senior experts retrieving a specific fact or project assumption, getting back a concise, source-grounded answer they can check.

The format to post in the code track channel: observation, North Star, hypothesis, user, input, output.

05Ten golden pairs before one line of code

One input and one output is an example, not enough to build against. Write at least 10 to 20 pairs: the exact text a real user would type, and the output that would solve their problem. If you are the user, write them yourself and check them with people like you. This is the golden dataset; it returns in the probabilistic module for evals and fine-tuning.

Recall the Lecture 1 game: to verify an app that adds two numbers, you write 1 + 2 = 3 and a few more, and compare. The golden dataset is what tells you whether your verifier will be deterministic, probabilistic or a human in the loop.

06The verifier is a threshold, worked in minutes

Say the email step is Aarav's biggest time sink: 15 minutes a day by hand. With the plan it takes 5. That saves 10 a day, 50 minutes over a 5-day week, close to the 1 hour bar. The plan passes. If the plan only cuts it to 10 minutes, that is 25 minutes a week, and it fails.

A good output clears a number you wrote down before you built anything. Without a threshold it is a demo, not impact.

The written verifier for Aarav's first golden pair asked for four things in the output: a metric with a baseline, the named bottleneck, a test on past input with known answers, and a list of what is out of scope.

07Test-driven development, written in English

Test-driven development (TDD) says write the test before the software; once the software passes your codified tests, you know it works. Hard to keep up, so few people do. What changes now is the language: most of your tests will be in English.

Almost everyone uses AI, utilization sits under 20%, and the impact on real earnings is in single digits. Building without a verifier is how that gap stays wide. Mask anything proprietary (percentages, or X, Y, Z) but share the spec, because that is what makes feedback possible.

08Two systems, one interface

Two systems: Aarav, and a workflow plan generator that can run on a machine. Anupma named what sits between: a user interface, with a common language (English, text fields) and rules (the web's HTTP). Golden pair 1, written live:

"I'm a business analyst at a mid-sized IT company in Pune. Every Friday, I make the weekly status report for our client. I open Jira, filter tickets closed this week, copy them into a Word template, then go through email and Slack to find blockers... Takes around 3 hours. My manager usually edits the risk section because I miss things."

Input type: text. Output type: text. So you can run a manual MVP before building anything: take Aarav's description on a call, write the plan yourself (with or without ChatGPT), send it, and see if it saves him time.

09The vote that lost

InterfaceVotesWhat the replay showed
Search box17No memory, no context. A follow-up means retyping the whole description, and an AI plan may come back different each run.
Form3Fields Aarav does not need, and no way to ask about the plan once it arrives.
Chat13Holds context, supports follow-ups until Aarav is satisfied. The winner.

Siddhant replayed the golden output, a day-by-day build plan with time saved as the metric, and Aarav asked: "What does backtesting mean?" How do you ask Google a follow-up? Swasti named the cost: remember the plan, retype, search again. Anupma named the missing property: no memory, no context. Bhavani added that it does not look like a conversation.

Pick the interface after you replay the user's conversation with your ideal output, never before.

Rule of thumb: about 90% of first problems fit one of three. One query, one answer: search. Many inputs: form. Follow-ups that need memory: chat. Build the cheapest one first and add nothing until it is verified. That is the V0, or MVP.

10Vibe code or first principles: the difference is control

Vibe code it with one prompt to Claude Code, Lovable, OpenAI or Gemini. Or write it from first principles in Python, AI allowed, understanding what every line does and why. Both produce a working chat. Tonight's task: do both and write a three-line review of what each gave you.

11Why Python, and why Gradio

The web is HTML; styling and scripting grew around it, and Python was never part of it. Until about 2021 Siddhant taught UI in JavaScript, HTML and CSS, and beginners had to learn three languages to ship one thing.

LibraryUse it for
GradioChat, forms, search-style inputs: any interface without heavy charts. Siddhant's default. Part of Hugging Face; AUTOMATIC1111, the Stable Diffusion UI with about 165,000 GitHub stars, runs on it.
StreamlitData-heavy front ends: dashboards and charts that update. Comes later.

12Four lines that are the whiteboard

The Gradio Hello World takes a name and returns a greeting:

import gradio as gr

def greet(name):
    return "Hello " + name

demo = gr.Interface(fn=greet, inputs="text", outputs="text")
demo.launch()

The function argument is the input. What follows return is the output. The body is the process, here almost nothing. gr.Interface is the interface: it declares input and output types, the common language and the rules. fn means function. For chat, swap the interface. Gradio's own example just echoes you:

import gradio as gr

def respond(message, history):
    return "You typed: " + message

gr.ChatInterface(fn=respond).launch()

Learn Gradio from its documentation, not from an LLM. Asking Claude gets you an answer; the docs get you the rabbit holes in the left rail (website widgets, Slack bots, Discord bots).

13Setting up, and the errors you should want tonight

  1. Install Python from python.org.
  2. Install one IDE: Google Antigravity, Cursor or VS Code. An IDE is a notepad for code where you read and review before you push anything anywhere. VS Code is free; Antigravity is free with generous limits; Cursor has a paid plan for its models.
  3. Make a folder, open it in the IDE, create app.py and paste the Hello World.
  4. Run pip install gradio, then python app.py, and open the local link it prints.

If def, return and functions are unfamiliar, finish the Big Binary Python prerequisite first; lectures will not teach Python syntax. The next office hour reviews your work and your errors, then puts it on a shareable link.

14From the Q&A

  • Trackers in, exception table out: form or chat? (Charu) No answer without the spec. Walk your user's journey the way we walked Aarav's, then ask.
  • My project is building WhatsApp agents. (Kandarp) That is solution-first. Start from observation, input, output and process.
  • My hypothesis changes after every interview. Then do not build yet. To learn the process, use Aarav's documents. Spend real build hours only on a problem you can test with real people.
  • First time coding, from marketing. (Saiteja) You have company: across cohorts the split moved to about 60-40 first-time coders. Siddhant's own start: in a first-year break, one app a day, end to end, on a link, for 30 days. Today's is Hello World.
  • Not sure my problem is big or monetizable. (Mayuresh) It does not have to be. A Cohort 6 non-coder built 100+ apps in 3 months and has since raised funding. Volume compounds only if the projects point the same way.
  • Should the North Star be a product? No. It is a direction. Most reduce to a better job (define the role) or building something of your own (define what and for whom), six months out.

15What to do next

  1. Post your spec in the code track channel: observation, North Star, hypothesis with a number, one named user, input, output.
  2. Write 10 golden pairs with a real user, plus one verifier with a baseline and a threshold.
  3. Replay one pair and write the follow-up your user will ask. Pick search, form or chat from it.
  4. Get Hello World running locally in Gradio, then the chat echo. Log every error and how you fixed it.
  5. Build the chat twice (vibe coded and first principles) and write the three-line review.

All five are in the practice set.

Keep this

You do not choose an interface. You derive it from the output your user needs. Write the ideal input and ideal output first, replay what your user will do with that output, and the interface falls out of the replay. The room voted 17 to 13 for a search box, and one follow-up question overturned the vote. Code is the last step, and in Gradio it is four lines that restate the whiteboard.
