Aarav, from observation to MVP plan

One full run of the Discovery to MVP coach, condensed. Read what each phase produced, then run it on your own observation.

The one thing to keep

Every phase must produce evidence or surface an assumption. If it does neither, it is theatre. Prove the outcome by hand with real people before any software exists: if humans can deliver it, you know what to automate; if they cannot, software would not have saved you.

01How the Coach Works

  1. Entry gate. Do you have a real observed problem, not a solution you like? If not, run the OPT Coach first.
  2. Discovery, phases 1 to 5. Observation, subject, evidence audit, causal chain, a hypothesis that can fail.
  3. Stress test, phase 6. Rank the riskiest assumptions, a Mom Test interview, three synthetic personas.
  4. Manual MVP, phase 7. A 21-day pilot by hand: who, what, price, metrics, success criteria, kill conditions.
  5. Output. A discovery document and a manual MVP plan. About 90 to 150 minutes, one question at a time.

The one rule: no simulated outcomes

The coach simulates a practice interview and practice personas, because those teach a skill. It never simulates the result of your pilot. A fake "Cohort 1 report" is fluent fiction built from your own assumptions. Twenty-one real days teach more than twenty-one simulations.

02The User: Aarav

26, Pune. B.Tech from a tier-2 college, three years as a business analyst at an IT services company. Not non-technical, not deeply technical. It is 12:38 am and he has spent eleven minutes on one resume bullet that describes work AI already does.

He has tried everything: ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, n8n, agent tutorials. He has 11 saved startup ideas, 3 half-finished projects, 19 bookmarked tutorials and a folder called "AI Projects Final" with nothing final in it. The loop: anxiety → tutorial → tool → idea → overthinking → more anxiety.

His problem is not laziness or information. It is high ambition, low clarity. He does not need another tool or 100 ideas. He needs one real workflow worth solving, and proof he can build for it.

03The Discovery Document

1 · Observation

Early-career professionals in urban India arrive AI-aware and motivated, but fail at selection, scoping and first proof. They see AI at the category level ("sales automation") and skip defining the user, the repeated task, the bottleneck, the inputs, the outputs and the success metric. Every project starts vague and collapses back into consumption: many saved ideas, zero shipped proof.

2 · Subject

Who22 to 30, urban India, graduates in early roles: analyst, junior dev, marketer, ops, freelancer.
Behaviour3 to 7 AI videos a week, 5 to 15 saves, 1 to 2 new tools. Shipping close to zero.
Money₹4L to ₹20L salary. Would pay ₹15,000 to ₹40,000 for clarity, structure and proof, not content.
FeelingLate, scattered, unconverted. Buys after a rupture: a peer lands an AI role, a manager asks him to build.
ConstraintsCannot quit, cannot spend a year on fundamentals, needs visible progress fast.
Stage3 to 9 months in the loop.

Smallest win in 60 to 90 days: one specific workflow, mapped, its bottleneck found, a small AI-assisted version tested on real inputs, presented as a demo and case study.

Out of scope: passive learners with no urgency, and validated founders who need scale, not diagnosis.

3 · Evidence audit

4 · Causal chain

  1. AI changes fast → pressure to upgrade, urgency without direction.
  2. The internet rewards novelty → option overload.
  3. No method to diagnose a workflow → he cannot filter options; every idea stays vague. Intervention point.
  4. Vague idea → no boundary for the build → scope collapse.
  5. Building feels risky → back to tutorials → motion without proof.
  6. No artifact → self-image stays "learner".
  7. Any trigger (a peer's demo, a manager's ask) restarts the loop.

Only link 3 is addressed. Saying which links you will not touch is part of the answer.

5 · Hypothesis

Falsifiable

We believe AI-aware early-career professionals with no shipped proof are stuck because they lack a method to turn broad AI possibility into one scoped workflow. If we provide a workflow diagnosis system and a guided build sprint, at least 60% of active students will map one real workflow, build a working AI-assisted version and present a credible demo within 90 days.

What would prove it wrong, and what it does not claim
Wrong if
  • Fewer than 40% produce a credible demo in 90 days.
  • Students finish diagnosis but still do not build: execution is the real bottleneck.
  • Students do not see the proof as a career win.
  • Students mainly want jobs or income, not proof.
  • Students cannot find real workflows even with guidance.
Does not claim

Jobs, placements or revenue; that one proof ends the anxiety; that diagnosis removes execution difficulty; that coders, no-coders and founders need the same build help.

7 · Top risks going into the MVP

  • Students diagnose and still do not build. Then the product needs a heavier build sprint.
  • The proof does not land as a career win without repackaging.
  • Aarav avoids talking to real users because it feels riskier than another video. If fewer than 6 finish their conversations by Day 5, the thesis is revised.

04The Manual MVP Plan

21 days, 10 users, ₹21,000. No software built.

Who

Past 100xEngineers students and first and second degree connections. One screening question:

"Tell me about the last AI project you seriously tried to build. What triggered it, what did you do first, where exactly did you stop, and what did you do after?"

Accept only a specific stalled attempt. Reject "I've been exploring AI", validated founders and passive learners. Screen 12 to 14 to land 10.

What, day by day

DaysActionOutput
0Paid intake and screening10 paid students
1 to 2Diagnosis call, then rank 5 to 7 real workflows on frequency, pain, access, AI fit, proof speedOne workflow, one user
3 to 5Talk to 2 people in that workflow. No pitching, only study the current processReal evidence
6 to 7Map the workflow, scope the smallest AI version. Gate: one user, task, bottleneck, input, output, metricOne-page build brief
8 to 14Build sprint, code or no-code. Works on 3 realistic inputsTestable prototype
15 to 16Show it to 1 to 2 real users; write a before-and-after proof statementProof of value
17 to 21Case study, 3 to 5 minute Loom, review, Demo Day"AI Workflow Proof 01" folder

Missed the Day 7 gate: a 20-minute rescue call (narrow to a weekly task, take a standard archetype, or step back to observer). Still unscoped by Day 9: out of the sprint.

Price

₹21,000 upfront. Full refund until Day 2 if no viable workflow is found. No refunds after Day 3. Did every step and still no proof: 7 more days of support, free.

Measure, at n = 10WeakMixedStrong

Strong (3+ strong, none weak): run Cohort 2 at ₹35,000. Mixed: fix the phase that broke, rerun with 10. Weak (2+ weak): stop and go back to the evidence audit.

Kill conditions, checked during the pilot

TripwireWhenWhat it means
Fewer than 7 paidBefore Day 1The offer is not compelling. Do not start.
Fewer than 6 log 2 real conversationsDay 5The bottleneck is avoiding real users.
Fewer than 6 scoped after rescueDay 9The diagnosis method is weak.
Fewer than 5 submit any prototypeDay 14Execution is the bottleneck. Thesis wrong.

If one trips, stop and write it down. Do not improvise a rescue; it is the most useful data the pilot produces.

Cohort 2: is the founder the product?

Two to three weeks later, a senior mentor runs the same playbook. The founder trains them, reviews recordings and attends Demo Day silently, but never rescues a student. If completions drop more than 20%, the founder was carrying the outcome: turn what they did into scripts and rubrics, and rerun. Cohort 1 must hand over every template, script and rubric it used.

What the pilot is explicitly not doing
  • No new software, no automation of delivery, no course, no platform.
  • No students without a recent stalled attempt; no passive learners or validated founders.
  • No promises of jobs, income or revenue.
  • No building before a scoped workflow; no skipping user conversations.
  • No dashboards, logins, databases, agents or full SaaS. No polish.
  • No prototype counts without user feedback; no case study without a working demo.
  • No moving the success criteria once the pilot starts, and no scaling before n = 10 produces evidence.

The sentence at the end

Whatever the outcome, this pilot will tell us whether 10 genuine Aaravs will pay to go from AI confusion to one real workflow proof in 21 days, or whether the real bottleneck is deeper than workflow diagnosis.

05Now Yours

  1. Run the coach on your own observation
  2. Write your hypothesis that can fail
  3. Run your manual MVP by hand for Assignment 1

Aarav's pilot is a founder's plan with 10 paying users. Assignment 1 is smaller: 2 to 5 real people, by hand, in 21 days. Keep the same discipline: criteria and kill conditions written before Day 1.
