Assignment 1 (mandatory) · Week 0 · Both tracks

First Proof

21 days. One observation from your own life. One link a stranger can use.

Follows Live Lecture 01: Idea to App WorkshopDue Fri 9 Oct 2026

The brief

Fix your North Star, write an observation from your own lived experience, run discovery-to-mvp-coach to a falsifiable hypothesis and a manual MVP plan, publish a tracker for that plan, then deliver the value by hand to 2 to 5 real people and write down the input, process and output you used.

01Why this exists

  • Problem first. Most products fail because someone built a real solution to a problem nobody had. Investigate the problem, attack your own assumptions, and run a cheap experiment before you build anything.
  • Aarav was a proxy. The skill is finding the one workflow in your own experience worth solving, proving part of its value by hand, and only then deciding what to automate. Weeks 1 to 8 teach the automation; they do not make sense without knowing what to automate.
  • Manual on purpose. The output is not an app. It is a process you have run once by hand, with a number attached, and a hypothesis you now know to be truer or falser than it was.

02The five steps

  1. Fix the North Star. Six-month timeline, role and identity, level or income or scale, and the specific thing that bridges them. Test: could someone else verify it on the date? Absolute number, or relative to a baseline you have measured. One goal, not four.
  2. Write the observation. One real person, one real situation, at the depth of what is on their desk at 12:38 AM, what they do, what breaks, how long it takes. Your lived experience, not a borrowed story, with no solution in it. It points toward your North Star; it is where the North Star's specific thing comes from.
  3. Run discovery-to-mvp-coach. Install the skill file (Settings, Customise, Skills, upload) and invoke it with /discovery-to-mvp-coach. Paste the observation. Budget 60 to 90 minutes. Answer every question yourself and add assumptions the skill missed. Do the Phase 6 simulated interviews properly and keep the 4 to 5 questions it grades you on. Save both outputs: the discovery document and the MVP plan.
  4. Publish the tracker. Paste the MVP plan into Lovable (free credits on a new account) or any AI builder and ask for a tracker of every line item in the 21-day pilot. Preview, then Publish. The link must open for a stranger without your help. Note what breaks: it is material for the next two weeks.
  5. Run the manual MVP and write the process. Deliver the value by hand to 2 to 5 real people in the first 14 days, asking Mom Test questions: their life, their past, specifics, emotions. Fill the card in section 03 for what you actually did. Update the tracker daily.

03Input, process, output

Every piece of software reduces to these three words.

FieldWhat to writeStand-up example
InputThe raw material, its format, where it comes from, how often it arrives. Attach one real sample.Messy transcript of the weekly Zoom stand-up, one file per call
OutputWhat the recipient gets, its format, who receives it, and the decision it lets them make.One-page summary of last week's calls, emailed to the founder, brief enough to act on
ProcessEvery step you took by hand to turn the input into the output, in order, including judgment calls and how you made them.Paste transcript into an LLM; prompt for summary; format; send by email; when two updates conflict, check Jira and keep the later one
Time and cost todayMinutes per run before your process, and what an hour of that person costs.Founder or assistant, about 3 hours a week
Time and cost afterMinutes per run with your manual process, measured on at least two real runs.15 minutes to review and sign off
MetricRevenue increased or cost and time saved. One number, with its unit.Roughly $300 a week saved per person at $100 an hour
Cannot yet be done by handSteps you could not perform manually and why. These are your open questions for weeks 1 to 8.Pulling from three systems with conflicting values

Do not automate anything on this card during the 21 days. The card is the input to Week 1, where the module turns it into a system: deterministic parts that do exactly what they are told, probabilistic parts that use an LLM, and parts that stay human.

04Rules of the build

  • Manual first. No agents, no automation, no product. Use an LLM by hand if it helps you do a step, and write down the prompt as part of the process.
  • Your observation, not a borrowed one. "People want to automate workflows so I will build five agents" is rejected on sight.
  • The hypothesis must be able to fail. Subject, problem, cause, intervention, a number, a deadline, and the result that would prove you wrong.
  • Real people, not only the simulation. Phase 6 in the skill is practice. It does not count toward the user research conversations.
  • One cycle at a time. Around 90% of the room may change idea by Week 2. Run one observation to the end of validation before starting the next. If it dies, that is a result: log it and rerun the skill on the next one.
  • LLM assistance. The skill, summaries of The Mom Test, and Lovable are the tools of the exercise. Do not generate the observation with an LLM: it is the one input that is in no training set, and the only part that is yours.

05What to submit

DeliverableWhat it must show

ObservationOne real person, one real situation, real numbers, no solution. Aligned to the North Star's specific thing.

VerdictTwo lines: is the hypothesis truer or falser than on day 0, and what you are changing.

Submit through the assignment form on the LMS. Share your tracker link early in the assignments channel: the sooner it is shared, the sooner it gets feedback. Stuck on the North Star, the observation, or testing a hypothesis by hand? Open a support ticket.

06After submission

Week 1 begins with your Input-Process-Output card, not with a hypothetical. Over the next two weeks the tracker gets a database and a backend built from first principles; over Weeks 1 to 8 the process on your card becomes a system of deterministic and probabilistic parts. None of that lands without a process you have already run by hand.

Cohort zero was 10 people, Rs 21,000 and 21 days with no software, testing one hypothesis with a 60% bar. Nine of ten shipped a credible demo. That was the first proof.
