Post-read notes · Idea to App workshop · Week 0

Observation before idea

A 21-day journey from discovery to a minimum viable product. What was said, in the order the room heard it. Read once end to end, then come back to sections 03, 09 and 11 while you do Assignment 1.

Fri 18 Sep 2026Instructor: Siddhant GoswamiBoth tracks

01The room: 1,400 years of experience

  • The Launchpad numbers. 66% working professionals, median 8+ years of experience. 68% from outside tech, across 57 industries. About 1,400 years of experience in one room.
  • Applied AI is industry agnostic. Everyone in your field says "I cannot build software". The first one to codify their judgment and scale it wins.
  • That experience is the raw material for the next eight weeks.

02A North Star has four elements

Two North Stars were pulled apart on the whiteboard. Kaushik: by December 2026, a dental tourism company running fully on AI agents except the patient calls, at $500 a month recurring. A C6 student: by December 2026, an AI real estate consultant in the UAE automating lead generation for agencies, at $15,000 a month.

  • Timeline. A date, six months out. A North Star for this cohort, not for your life.
  • Role and identity. What people will call you. "Leading an AI-powered engineering workflow" is a task, and begs the question of who leads it.
  • Level, income or scale. The quantity you will have on the date. Money, a designation, an appraisal or a measured impact, as long as it is a number.
  • The specific thing. The one workflow that bridges the role to the number. For the UAE consultant, automating lead gen. Almost everyone left this out.

Three from chat, reviewed live:

  • Anand. Timeline and a task, no identity. "100x productivity" with no baseline to measure against.
  • Prateek. "Build the top 5 AI use cases at my organisation." A constraint (a quarter of a 75-person team using them) in place of an outcome, and a specific thing that was still generic.
  • Saiteja. Passed all four: growth marketer or indie maker, marketing the AEO tool he already built, to 1K MRR.

The North Star defines the goal. It never explains the route.

03The mom test for a North Star

  • Send the sentence to your mother, or anyone older who does not know the jargon. On the date, can she verify whether it happened?
  • Saiteja: yes. 1,000 MRR is a bank statement or an invoice.
  • Anand: no. Nobody can check "100x productivity", because there is no number for today.
  • So the scale is absolute, or relative to a baseline you have already measured and written down. "100x better" with no baseline is a wish.
  • It may evolve, never stay vague. When you reach it (Saiteja may hit 1K MRR next month), rewrite it.

04The path is a proof, not a plan

  • No standard path exists. Build the app, build agents, take another course: everyone has their own answer. Module 2 borrows from research, which works by building evidence.
  • Stage 2 is a proof: the smallest demonstration that 0.1% of the value already exists. For Saiteja, the first $10 or $20 his tool earns, not a marketing strategy.

05Week 0, and why the order changed

  • Weeks 1 to 8 teach how to build: LLMs, making them trustworthy, harnesses, loops, agents, fine-tuning.
  • Week 0 answers three questions first. What is worth building (discovery). What the manual version looks like (the MVP). What in it should be automated (the process).
  • Six cohorts of evidence. Until C6 the capstones were low impact. Students could build. They did not know what to build.
  • So no artificial problem solved live while you watch. You solve yours. No clear North Star yet? Fill the Launchpad form and ask for a review.

06Aarav at 12.38 AM: what an observation looks like

It is 12.38 AM in Pune. Aarav is at his desk with three tabs open: a YouTube video called Top 10 AI Agent Projects to Build in 2026, an n8n automation tutorial, and his own resume. He has been staring at one bullet point for 11 minutes.

  • Who. 26, Tier 2 BTech, three years a business analyst at a mid-size IT services company. Neither non-technical nor deeply technical.
  • What he has tried. ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Cursor, n8n, every agent tutorial. 11 startup ideas in Notion, 3 finished, 19 tutorials bookmarked, a folder called AI Projects Final with nothing final in it.
  • At work. His manager wants "AI productivity" because the client asked. Everyone makes a deck. Nobody knows what to build.
  • The diagnosis. High ambition, low clarity. Anxiety, tutorial, tool, idea, overthinking, more anxiety. He needs one real workflow worth solving.
  • It came from sitting next to Aarav, day after day. It is not in any model's training data. What you have lived and nobody else has is the only moat left.
  • An observation is a detailed record of a real person, from your own experience, with no solution in it. "People want to automate workflows, so I will build five agents" is rejected on day one.
  • Your North Star tells you where to look. Saiteja's says: sit with growth marketers and watch what breaks.

07Story to subject to evidence to causal chain

A story can be wrong. The discovery-to-mvp-coach skill asks questions for 60 to 90 minutes and turns it into something checkable. Four moves, on Aarav's own document:

  • Compress the observation into a paragraph about a segment: ambitious early-career professionals in urban India, AI-aware and motivated, who fail at selection, scoping and the first proof.
  • Define the subject. Where they live, their role, their behaviour: AI content daily in bursts, 5 to 15 posts saved a week, one or two new tools tried a day. Demographic, behavioural, financial, psychological and structural markers.
  • Audit the evidence. What already confirms what you saw? Passive consumption turning active across every cohort; MOOCs with very high enrolment and very low completion.
  • Write the causal chain, labelled as assumptions. AI changes fast, so urgency without direction. The feed rewards novelty, so overload. No mental model to filter, so every idea feels possible. Stacked: the builder's anxiety.

08A hypothesis must be able to fail

  • A hypothesis is your belief about what the problem is, how you would solve it, and how you would know you were wrong. The last clause is the point: maybe the observation is bias.
  • Aarav's. Ambitious early-career professionals in urban India who are AI-aware but have no shipped proof are stuck because they lack a method to turn broad AI possibility into one workflow worth solving. With a structured workflow diagnosis and a guided sprint, at least 60% of active students will find one real user, map one repeated workflow, build a working AI-assisted version and present a credible demo within 90 days.
  • What kills it. Fewer than 60% of 250. Under 40% with a credible demo. Students finish diagnosis but still do not build. They want placement, not proof (a different buyer). They cannot find real workflows even with guidance (the gap is upstream).
  • Run the skill rather than the template alone. Subject, problem, cause, intervention, a number, a deadline, and what kills it.

09Stress test: the Mom Test, simulated

  • Phase 6 of the skill forces the practice most students skip. It lists every assumption so far (add your own), asks your confidence in each, then plays a person from your segment. In Aarav's run: Rohan Verma, 27, business analyst, an hour of AI content a day. You interview him.
  • The rules, from Rob Fitzpatrick's The Mom Test. Read the whole book.
  • Talk about their life, not your idea.
  • Ask about the past, not the future.
  • Listen for specifics, not generalities.
  • Dig on emotions, not opinions.
  • Then it grades you: strong moves, missed questions, what to listen for. You leave with four or five questions for a real conversation.
  • Practice, not a replacement. Real interviews are mandatory: 2 to 5 people minimum.

10Cohort zero: 10 people, Rs 21,000, 21 days

  • Phase 7 is a manual MVP plan: who you recruit, what you deliver, what you measure, what you charge.
  • The one on screen was 100xEngineers' own. Ten people, Rs 21,000 each, 21 days, no software, to test the hypothesis above. The bar was 60%. Nine of ten presented a credible demo. One later raised Rs 1 crore from NVIDIA and Startup India. The 3-month and then 6-month programmes grew from what those ten said.
  • The counter-example. Earlier workshops handed out five trending ideas to paste into a builder. It produced a pile of AI slop and value for nobody. Two of the instructor's businesses failed before this one for lack of a standard process. This is that process.

If you cannot test it by hand, do not build it. Do it by hand once, then decide what to automate.

11Input, process, output

  • Every piece of software, and every SOP, is three words. Write down the inputs, write down the outputs, define the process between them. The manual MVP is where you find yours.
  • The weekly stand-up. A 15-person team, too many updates for one founder, so an assistant was hired. Input: the messy transcript. Output: a summary brief enough to decide on, by email. Process: paste into an LLM, summarise, format, send.
  • No tool needed. Resist agents. Do the most manual thing that works, once, and write down what you did, including how you judged when two sources disagreed. "I used my judgment" is the rabbit hole; how you judge is the question.
  • From next week it becomes a system. Some parts deterministic: "if the transcript length is 0, print error 500" does so every time. Some probabilistic: an LLM may answer differently each call. Which is which, and what cannot be automated, is weeks 1 to 8.

12Where is the app? A tracker, not a product

  • The MVP is manual, so there is nothing to build yet. The link is a personal dashboard.
  • Paste your MVP plan into Lovable (or Google AI Studio, any builder) and ask for a tracker of every line item in the 21-day pilot. Check the preview, click Publish, share the link.
  • No backend, no database yet. Whatever breaks is a learning item for the next two weeks.
  • Update it daily, which means doing the work daily.

13From the Q&A

Charu's Friday report. North Star: by March 2027, an AI-enabled project manager with up to a 100% increase in total compensation. Timeline, identity and income present; the specific thing missing. Her observation supplied it: every Friday a PM pulls from several systems to build a status report, and when the facts differ she cannot tell which is right, though she answers for what leadership reads.

  • Put a number on it. 3 hours a week at $100 an hour is $300 a week per PM. Cut to a 15-minute review and that saves $250 to $300 a week per PM, compounding across the team.
  • The observation must align with the North Star, and in doing so supplies the specificity it lacked.

Only two metrics move compensation: revenue increased, or cost and time saved. Pick the one your observation touches and put a number on it.

A student who replaced a $10,000 Slack bill with a configured open-source messenger had a proof his manager could not ignore, and a freelance offer for every other company paying the same bill.

  • Time budget. 8 to 10 hours a week, about 1 to 1.5 hours a day. No upper limit.
  • How long validation takes. The skill, 40 to 90 minutes. Four calls of 15 to 20 minutes each. 3 to 4 hours in all; double it for safety.
  • Pivoting. Expected. Around 90% may change their idea by week 2. Run one cycle to the end before starting another.
  • Several incentives. Mohit's North Star had four. Keep one, and add the industry: that is where the observation lives.
  • "What are you charging". A validation device. If the problem is internal to your team, write not applicable and keep the time-saved metric.
  • Competitors already exist. Sai's question, why validate when paying customers exist: answered with data in the orientation.
  • Certifications and portfolios are proxies. Clarity on your specific thing beats another certificate.

14What to do next

  1. Rewrite your North Star with all four elements and run the mom test on it. No North Star yet? Fill the Launchpad form first.
  2. Write your observation at the depth of the Pune story: a real person, a real desk, real numbers, no solution.
  3. Run discovery-to-mvp-coach (skill file on the LMS; Settings, Customise, Skills, then /discovery-to-mvp-coach). 60 to 90 minutes. Keep the discovery document and the MVP plan.
  4. Publish a 21-day tracker from the MVP plan in Lovable or any builder.
  5. Start the manual MVP. Speak to 2 to 5 real people with the questions the simulation gave you, and write down the input, process and output of what you delivered by hand. Full brief in Assignment 1.

Keep this

The idea does not come first. The observation does, from your own lived experience, aimed by a North Star with four elements and written as a hypothesis that can be proven false. Nothing gets automated until you have done it by hand once and written down the input, the process and the output.
