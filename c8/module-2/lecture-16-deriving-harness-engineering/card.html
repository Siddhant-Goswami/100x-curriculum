Your harness card

Take one problem of your own. Write the five lines around its LLM. Find one answer it got wrong. Add the one part that catches it.

On your own, about an hour. Any chat with an LLM is enough. Submit on the LMS before Lecture 17, Fri 4 Dec.

Done equals

Five lines, three questions with their ideal answers, one wrong answer you kept, and the score (right, wrong, held back) before and after you added one part.

01Your system

The problem you ran the four steps on after Lecture 15.

No problem yet? Write three questions people really ask you at work, each with the answer you would give. Your second brain works too, and so does any app you built in this cohort.

You need no code and no running app. Any chat with an LLM is the LLM. You can be every other part by hand: you paste what it reads, you fetch what it asks for, you check the answer.

02Draw it

Draw every box between the question going in and the answer coming out. Paper is fine. Give each box one of three colours.

  • Follows rules: a search, a file, a database, a tool, a rule
  • Predicts: the LLM
  • A person: you, your user, a reviewer

This is the system on the Harness Bench, drawn the same way.

03Fill five lines

One line each. Two questions sort the first three lines.

  1. Was it given to the LLM before it wrote, or fetched because the LLM asked for it? Fetched is Asks.
  2. If it was given: is it written down from earlier use and kept for next time (Remembers), or is it the instructions and pages for this question (Reads)?

One thing can sit on two lines. The 100x brain is memory someone wrote down (Remembers) that the LLM reaches by asking for a page (Asks).

Doing it by hand counts: if you paste the policy in yourself, that is Reads, done by you. "Nothing" is an honest answer, and usually the most useful one.

04Find one it got wrong

  • Write three questions real people ask, each with its ideal answer. Make at least one of them judgment-based.
  • Ask each one in a new chat. Use a temporary or incognito chat if your app has one, and leave web search off, so the app's own memory and search stay out. Give the LLM only what your five lines say it has.
  • Mark each answer right, wrong, or held back (held back means a check you wrote stopped it before you used it). That is your "before". Your ideal answers are how you score. They are not a part of the system.
  • Keep one wrong answer. Write it on the card.

All three right? Then the questions are too easy. Add one whose answer is only in your head. You are the only one who can mark it, which is the Verifier's Law from Lecture 1.

05Add one part

Look at the wrong answer, then at your five lines. The cause usually sits on the line that says "nothing".

Add one part there, by hand. Paste the page that question needed (Reads). Or keep a note of what was decided and why after each chat, and paste it in at the start of the next one (Remembers). Or write one rule an answer must pass, and hold back any answer that fails it instead of using it (Checks). Draw the new box on your picture in its colour.

Ask the same three questions again, each in a new chat, and score them the same way. A part earns its place if more answers are right, or fewer wrong ones get through. A check often does the second: the right count stays the same and a wrong answer is held back. If neither number moves, say so on the card. That part has not earned its place yet.

06Submit it on the LMS

Put the drawing, the card and your three questions in one file, with a photo of the drawing if you drew it on paper. Submit it on the LMS before Lecture 17, Fri 4 Dec 2026, and have it open in Lecture 17.
