Learn design by designing this page

This page has no design yet. Just words.

Each step asks a question. Guess the answer, then click. The page changes, and the changes add up.

What is the first thing to work on?

Content

Not the look. The words. Design serves what it is applied to, and with nothing to show there is nothing to design.

So you write first, and the browser shows it like this: Times New Roman, lines to the edge, blue links. That is on purpose. Nothing gets added to this page until you can see why it is needed.

In the brief: Content first. Every screen starts as the words it has to carry.

Now read the paragraph above on a laptop.

What makes it hard?

Width

The line is too long. Your eye reaches the end, travels back, and loses its place.

The rule. One column, about 70 characters wide. Longer and reading becomes work.

In the brief: Width: one column, about 70 characters, centred.

Give the words a column.

Same words, shorter lines. Your eye comes back to the right place. But the letters still look like a court order.

Why does it still look like 1996?

Typeface

Look closely at the letters. Each stroke ends in a small foot, and the strokes swing from thick to thin. Times was drawn for ink on paper. On a screen the thin strokes break up.

The rule. One typeface, made for screens. You do not need to choose well. You need to choose once. Inter, if you have no opinion yet.

In the brief: Type: Inter, for everything.

Switch to a screen typeface.

Look again. No feet, even strokes, letters a little wider. Same words, and they read like now. Still tight, though.

What is tight?

Size and line height

The text is sized for a 2002 monitor, and each line sits on the one below.

The rule. Body text 16 pixels or more. Lines one and a half times their height apart. The gap is where your eye lands on the way back.

In the brief: Size: 18px body, line height 1.6.

Make the text bigger and give the lines room.

Bigger letters, a gap under every line. The words breathe. The paragraphs do not.

What tells you one thought has ended?

Space

Nothing does, yet. Space should. Things close together read as one group. Things apart read as different.

The rule. One unit, 8 pixels, and multiples of it. A little inside a group, a lot between groups.

In the brief: Space: 8px unit. 24px between paragraphs, 64px before a heading.

Put space between the ideas.

Each paragraph is its own block now, and each heading has room above it. Look at the headings: bigger, because the browser guessed.

What is first on this page?

Hierarchy

Nothing is. Title, section names, bold words: all of them shout a little, so none of them leads.

The rule. One thing first, biggest by a lot. Then two sizes only: the one thing, and everything else. Weight, not size, for what comes next.

In the brief: Hierarchy: one title at 40px, weight 700. Section headings 22px, weight 600. Nothing else changes size.

Make one thing first.

Scroll up. The title leads, the headings follow, everything else is quiet. But the page is still pure black on pure white.

Why does it tire the eye?

Colour

Pure black on pure white is the hardest contrast there is. After a page it vibrates. And the browser's blue points at nothing, because it was chosen for nothing.

The rule. Near-black on near-white, warmed a little. Then one colour, plus grey. Colour is a pointing finger. Colour everything and you point at nothing.

In the brief: Colour: ink #1B1B1B on #F7F6F2, secondary text #5A5A5A. One accent, #B8431F, for links and the one action only.

Soften the contrast and pick one colour.

Warm off-white, softer black, one colour on the links. Now look at the rules on this page.

What is off about them?

Alignment

Each one is pushed in by forty pixels. Two left edges, and the eye knows before you do.

The rule. One left edge. Mark a quote with a line, not an indent. Most of "something feels off" is two edges a few pixels apart.

In the brief: Layout: one left edge. Quotes get a thin left line, not an indent.

Line everything up.

One left edge now, and a thin line marks each rule. Every step on this page has had one action.

Can you find it?

The one action

Barely. It is dressed like every other link, so the most important thing on the screen looks like everything else.

The rule. One screen, one action, and it should look like one: filled with the accent, big enough for a thumb, alone on its line. Links stay underlined text.

In the brief: Action: one filled button per screen in the accent, 44px tall. Links stay underlined text. Other buttons outlined, grey.

Make the action look like one.

There it is, below. One filled button, easy to hit, hard to miss. Press it and the page snaps. Rest on it and nothing happens.

What is missing?

Motion

A reply. Motion says something responded, and where to look next. Everywhere, it is noise. Last and small, it is the difference between a page and a product.

The rule. Motion last, only in reply to the person. 150 to 250 milliseconds, slowing as it lands. Nothing moves on its own.

In the brief: Motion: 200ms ease-out on hover, press and reveal. Nothing moves unprompted. Respect reduced-motion.

Let it move a little.

Rest on the button below. It lifts, and gives when you press it. Ten decisions made. One more question: every other C8 notes page looks like this one.

Why?

The system

Because the decisions were written down once, in one stylesheet, and every C8 page loads it. This page has not loaded it yet.

The rule. Decide once, write it down, reuse it everywhere. That file is a design system. Everything else is detail.

Load the C8 stylesheet.

Same words. This page is now part of the notes you have been reading: header, contents, dark mode, all from one file.

You never dragged a box or picked a pixel. Every change was a sentence. Here they are together: design.md, the file you hand to the machine. Give it to Claude Code, v0 or Stitch with "make me a sign-up page". Where it comes back wrong, fix the sentence, not the pixels.


  
 

  
That is the whole loop. Look at good screens until you can name what they do. Write it down. Let the machine make it. Look again.
