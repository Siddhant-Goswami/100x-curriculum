Design for Non-Designers

You do not need to become a designer. You need to see what a designer sees, say it in words, and let the machine make it. Here is the sequence, and the few things worth your time at each step.

  1. Build your taste
  2. Learn the five rules
  3. Write the brief, not the pixels
  4. Add motion last
  5. Put it in front of someone

The definition

Design is not decoration, or the colour you pick at the end. It is deciding what a person sees first, second and third, so they act without thinking. Every screen you have liked did three things. One thing first. Room around it. Everything else quiet.

You already have taste. You know in a second whether an app feels cheap or right. What you lack is the words for why, and the habit of looking. Both can be built.

Step 1: build your taste

Taste is everything you have looked at closely, plus the words for why it worked. So before any tool, you look.

Ten minutes a day. Pick one screen from the libraries below and say out loud: what is first? What is quiet? Why does this one feel calm and that one loud? Noticing without naming is just scrolling.

Save the ones that stop you, not the famous ones. That folder is your taste, the one thing the machine cannot generate. The screens you cannot stop looking at point at what you should build.

Step 2: learn the five rules

Two weeks of looking and the same few things keep showing up. Nearly everything that looks designed is these five.

  1. 1
    One thing first.Every screen has one job. Make it the biggest, darkest, or only coloured thing.
  2. 2
    Space is not empty.Room around a thing says it matters. Use more than you think, in steps of 8px.
  3. 3
    Line things up.Pick one left edge. Most "something feels off" is two edges three pixels apart.
  4. 4
    One typeface, two sizes.Big for the one thing, small for the rest. Use weight for emphasis. Inter, if you have no opinion yet.
  5. 5
    One colour, plus grey.Colour is a pointing finger. Colour everything and you point at nothing.

You learn these in an afternoon. Seeing them break takes months. That is why step 1 never stops.

Step 3: write the brief, not the pixels

Most roadmaps end with "practise in a design tool for two years". Skip that. The machine has the hands now. Your job is the brief.

A brief is a short file, design.md, that says what good looks like: one colour, one typeface in two sizes, the spacing unit, the tone, what goes first. Hand it to Claude Code, Stitch or v0 and ask for a screen. Compare what comes back with your taste. Where it is wrong, fix the brief, not the pixels. Ask again.

A brief, for example: a small invoicing tool for freelancers

Feel: calm, like a well-kept notebook. Nothing shouts.
Colour: one accent, #1D4ED8, used only for the single primary action. Everything else is grey on white.
Type: Inter. 32px for the one heading on a screen, 16px for everything else. Weight 600 for emphasis, never colour.
Space: 8px unit. 24px between groups, 48px between sections. When in doubt, more.
Layout: one column up to 720px. Left edge is the only edge.
Every screen: one job, stated in the heading. The primary action is the only coloured thing.
Never: gradients, shadows deeper than 2px, more than one font, icons without labels.

That file is your taste, written down, and it compounds. Every screen teaches you one more line. After fifty, it is a design system, and you never opened the pen tool.

Step 4: add motion last

Motion is the last five percent and the first thing people notice. A badly laid out screen that moves is still badly laid out. Animate only once the still frame passes the five rules.

Then treat it like colour. One thing moves, to show where to look. Everything else holds still.

Step 5: put it in front of someone

A screen is finished when a stranger uses it without asking you how. Show one person. Say nothing. Watch where their eyes go first. If it is not the thing you put first, back to the brief.

Show, watch, change, repeat: that is what designers call process. The courses below give it more steps and better names. Read them once, then run the loop.

On taste

Nobody is born with taste. Every designer you admire spent years looking before they could see. You can do it faster, because a machine will make a hundred versions while you look.

What the machine cannot do is prefer. Choosing is your call, built from everything you have looked at and liked. The less you copy, the harder you are to replace. Your weird is your moat.
