100x Tutor

Turn a chatbot into a tutor that asks you the questions, so you can see what you really understood.

About 25 to 40 short replies per lecture, over as many sittings as you like.

Start here

Copy the tutor, paste it into a new chat, and fill in the last line:

Teach me [topic]. I'm [your background]. Use only these notes: [paste your notes].

01Why it's here

Following a lecture and being able to do it yourself are two different things. The gap usually shows up when you sit down to build.

A normal chat hands you the answer, so the AI does the thinking. The tutor asks instead. It finds out what you already know, asks one question at a time, and pushes on the part that is shaky.

Use it after a lecture, while the notes are fresh, or before an office hour to find out what to ask.

02How to use it

  1. Copy and paste. Open a new chat in Claude, paste the tutor, replace the brackets in the last line, and send.
  2. Answer in your own words. Guesses help, and "I don't know" is a useful answer. Both show the tutor where to start.
  3. Save before you stop. Type save state and keep the block it gives you.
  4. Pick it up later. In a new chat, paste the tutor again, then type resume from: followed by your saved block and your notes.

03What it looks like

An HR professional who doesn't code, working through the Lecture 9 notes.

You

Teach me the main ideas from this lecture. I'm from HR and I don't code. Here are the notes: [Lecture 9 notes]

Tutor

The lecture comes down to 4 ideas: rules vs. prediction, checking an answer, whether a machine can learn it, and your own job. First, a few quick questions to see where you're starting from.

Your payroll system calculates salaries. Your colleague asks ChatGPT, "Is this candidate a good culture fit?" Which of the two would you expect to give the same answer every time you ask? Why?

You

The payroll system I think, because it's just doing math. ChatGPT would probably give different answers because it's kind of looking up opinions from somewhere?

Tutor

You have the first half: payroll follows fixed rules, so the same input gives the same output. I'll come back to the "looking up opinions" part later, because I think the mechanism is a bit different from that.

A little later

Tutor

Suppose you wanted a machine to get good at judging hiring decisions. You could start with 10 years of hiring records, with nothing about how each hire turned out. Or a clear definition of a good hire, but no past records. Which would help more, and why?

You

Option 1 at first, because 10 years sounds like a lot. But there's nothing saying how each hire turned out, so it couldn't tell which ones were good? And with no records I'm not sure what it would practice on, so maybe neither is enough alone?

Tutor

You reasoned your way to the right answer. Records without outcomes can't teach right from wrong, and a definition without examples gives nothing to practice on. The lecture's term for this is "verifier first, data second."

05Good to know

  • Levels are practice feedback, not a grade. The target is 3 out of 5, which means you can do it with some help. Ask for full mastery if you want to go further.
  • Your practice set stays yours. The tutor will review your draft and show examples on other tasks. It won't write what you hand in.
  • It can be wrong. When it disagrees with the notes, go with the notes and bring the question to the office hour. Use the best model you have, because the cheapest one we tried taught a wrong idea in testing.
  • Tested on Claude. You can paste it into ChatGPT or Gemini, but we haven't tested it there.
